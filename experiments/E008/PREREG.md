# E008: The self-improving loop on a synthetic question stream

- Registered: 2026-10-12
- Owner seat: CTO
- Rests on: [self-improving loop note](../../research/engineering/104-self-improving-loop.md), [decision 0008](../../research/decisions/0008-self-improving-loop.md), [calibration methods](../../research/science/019-calibration-methods.md), [forecast scoring](../../research/science/030-forecast-scoring.md)
- Claim it earns or kills: no public claim (the stream is synthetic). It decides whether the daily `improve` step stays in the cycle ([decision 0008](../../research/decisions/0008-self-improving-loop.md)).

## Hypothesis

On a synthetic stream where the raw forecaster is overconfident, the loop's published forecasts have lower Brier than the raw forecasts on later questions, and on a stream where the raw forecaster is already calibrated the loop rarely promotes and does not raise Brier.

## Primary metric

Overconfident arm: mean Brier over question-days of the published forecasts minus the raw forecasts, for questions resolving on days 61 to 100, paired by seed. Negative favours the loop.

## Secondary metrics

- The same difference for the lean arm and for the calibrated arm.
- False promotion rate: the share of calibrated-arm seeds with at least one promotion.
- Promotions per seed and the day of the first promotion, per arm.

## Data

Fully synthetic, generated by `engine/rw/experiments.py` with seeds 2000 to 2049. Each seed runs 100 days. Each day 2 questions open; each is forecast on 5 consecutive days and resolves the day after its last forecast. Each question's true probability is `sigmoid(z)` with `z ~ Normal(0, 1.5)`, and its outcome is a Bernoulli draw from it. The raw forecast on a day is `sigmoid(k * (z + e) + c)` with `e ~ Normal(0, 0.3)` drawn per question-day. Arms: calibrated (`k = 1`, `c = 0`), overconfident (`k = 1.6`, `c = 0`), lean (`k = 1`, `c = 0.4`). The arms share each seed's questions, outcomes and noise. No model is involved, so no licence or contamination issue applies.

## Baseline

The raw forecast with no map, which is what the daily cycle publishes today. A skeptic picks it because a loop that cannot beat doing nothing should not run.

The loop runs exactly as the daily step does, with the `rw.improve` functions and these values: a Platt map fitted by weighted log loss with each question weighted equally and an L2 penalty of 0.01 toward identity, at least 50 training questions, a holdout of the 30 most recent questions resolved after the incumbent took effect, margin 0.005 Brier, the `data/protocol.json` day-block bootstrap, a promotion taking effect the next day.

## Sample size and stopping rule

50 seeds per arm, 100 days each, run once. 50 seeds matches E003 and E006, and gives a false promotion rate to within about 8 points at 95%. No seeds are added after looking.

## Analysis plan

95% interval for each mean difference by bootstrap over seeds (10,000 resamples, seed 7, the E003 procedure). The hypothesis is supported if the primary interval lies wholly below zero, the calibrated-arm interval is not wholly above zero, and the false promotion rate is at most 10%. Report every arm, every seed's rows and every metric. No region or tier splits: the stream is synthetic.

## Kill criterion

If the calibrated-arm interval lies wholly above zero or its false promotion rate exceeds 10%, the loop promotes noise: the `improve` step is removed from the daily cycle and decision 0008 is revisited before the gate is retuned under a new registration. If only the primary fails, the loop is harmless but useless at this data volume: it stays, and we say it has not shown a gain.

## Cost cap

No model calls and no spend. Compute only, a few minutes on one machine. The runner has no timeout, so the run is stopped by hand if it passes one hour.
