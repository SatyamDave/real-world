## Decision

Real World builds toward two durable assets and claims no third. (1) Proprietary outcome data: each marked message test is a row of message, idea, ideal consumer, engine intent per segment, baseline intent, the founder's gut, and the real outcome (Landed or Flopped, plus an optional metric), sealed before the outcome existed. A founder adds a row to a shared record only by an explicit, per-test opt-in at the moment of marking, off by default; the shared row holds the category (one of 0016 rule 6's categories), the sealed numbers, the outcome and the metric, and never the message text, the idea, the ICP description, the account id or the email. Withdrawing the opt-in removes the row from every later fit and benchmark; versions already promoted keep their evidence hash. Opted-in backtests enter the shared record labelled retrospective and are used only for benchmarks, never for a calibration holdout, never for E010 and never for the public record. (2) Pooled calibration and category benchmarks, which are a data network effect in the narrow sense (one founder's mark improves the next founder's test in that category) with a ceiling per category, and the sealed, externally anchored public record, which is a trust asset and not a network effect. Public share pages for sealed tests are distribution, not a moat. There is no credible physical component; the record's external timestamp anchor is the substitute, and verified outcomes pulled from a founder's own systems are the one real-world signal worth adding later. The product changes these require are built in this order: category on every test; the opt-in "Add this result to the shared record" on marks; the same opt-in for backtests; category benchmarks on results; a public page per sealed test, by the founder's choice; pooled per-category calibration through 0008's gate, after its own registered experiment and after `company/decisions-needed.md` item 14 is answered; marks as training targets for 0010's owned model, after a founder decision on consent.

## Options considered

| Option | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Claim the engine (27 segments, many simulated consumers, the Jev decision model) as the moat | It is what we built | Copyable: the method is published ([science/016](../science/016-silicon-samples.md)), the model is rented, and a plain prompt is a strong baseline ([0016](0016-icp-audiences.md)); "technical moats are mostly over" is the founder's premise |
| Keep every founder's marks private, pool nothing | No consent work; nothing to leak | Each founder's own data is too small to calibrate on (0008's gate needs 80 resolved cases per fit, `engine/rw/settings.py`); no founder benefits from another's mark, so there is no network effect at all |
| Pool every mark by default, opt out to leave | Fastest data growth | Pseudonymised data stays personal data under the ICO ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/pseudonymisation/)); founders' unreleased messages are confidential; a default the founder did not choose breaks the trust the record sells |
| Per-test opt-in to a shared record without message text (chosen) | Consent is explicit and per row; nothing identifying or confidential leaves the account; still yields calibration and benchmark data | Slower growth; opt-in rate unknown until measured; a map fitted on Jev intents may fall under TypeSafe MCA §2.3(b) (item 14) |
| Invent a physical component (hardware panel, in-store kiosks) | Matches the third moat on the list | No such product exists or is planned; it would be for show, which the founder's brief rules out |

## Why this one

- Data alone is a weak moat unless usage keeps producing fresh, proprietary data that resolves against reality ([092](../business/092-moat-theory.md), [a16z](https://a16z.com/the-empty-promise-of-data-moats/), [NFX](https://www.nfx.com/post/network-effects-manual)). A marked test is that: it exists only because a founder ran the test before shipping and came back after; no rival can rebuild it from a snapshot, and the seal proves the prediction came first.
- Every synthetic-research vendor asks buyers to trust its own accuracy claim; independent tests find 12-15 point errors per question ([market/003](../market/003-synthetic-research-vendors.md)). A pooled record of predicted against real outcomes, scored against a plain baseline, is the one thing those vendors do not publish.
- Calibration on outcomes is the documented fix for the failure modes simulated respondents share ([science/018](../science/018-failure-modes.md)); fine-tuning on human answers beat prompting by 32-46% on held-out subgroups ([science/105](../science/105-survey-finetuned-opinion-model.md)). 0008 and 0010 already define the gate and the owned model; this record supplies them with outcome data from the product.
- Opt-in without text keeps to what [product/privacy.md](../../product/privacy.md) promises today and to the ICO's warning that weakly anonymised data is still personal data.

## What would make us revisit it

- [E010](../../experiments/E010/PREREG.md) at 100 marked tests: the engine's Brier is not below the baseline's. Pooled calibration then fits the baseline's probability first, and the thesis that segments carry signal is dropped from every surface.
- Fewer than 1 in 10 founders who run a test mark it within 14 days (0016's trigger): there is no data asset, and this record is rewritten.
- After 300 marks, fewer than 30% are added to the shared record: no category reaches 0008's 80-case gate in a useful time, and pooling by default with an opt-out is reconsidered in a new record with legal review.
- A legal read of item 14 says a map fitted on Jev intents is not allowed: pooled calibration runs only on the baseline and on 0010's owned model.
- The first category to reach 80 shared marks fails 0008's promotion gate: the pooled-calibration claim is withdrawn until a later fit passes.
- A rival publishes a sealed, externally anchored record of consumer message tests with outcomes: the record is no longer unique, and the moat rests on data volume alone.
