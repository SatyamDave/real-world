## Answer

The deadline is confirmed: Winter 2027 applications close Monday November 2, 2026 at 8:00 p.m. PT, and on-time applicants hear by December 11. Real World launches October 27, which leaves six days between launch and submission. Treat the public track record, not the pitch, as the application: the product, the hashed forecasts and the first scored results should all exist before we submit.

## Findings

- Deadline and dates. The apply page says Winter 2027 runs January to March in San Francisco. Applications are due November 2 at 8:00 p.m. PT. On-time applications get a decision by December 11. Interviews are mostly by video in November and December. YC usually decides the same day as the interview and gives interviewees detailed feedback. [YC apply page](https://www.ycombinator.com/apply)
- Late applications are read, "Most late applications will get a decision within a month," with no guarantee, and they cannot be marked on time. No feedback is given unless you get an interview. [YC FAQ](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)
- The deal is $500,000: a $125,000 post-money SAFE for 7%, plus a $375,000 uncapped MFN SAFE, plus a pro rata right. No fees, no milestones. [YC deal page](https://www.ycombinator.com/deal)
- What YC says it wants. On average 40% of funded companies per batch are "just an idea." Revenue and prior funding are not required. The team must be able to build the product. Solo founders are accepted, but YC says co-founders succeed more often. [YC FAQ](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)
- Video. Neither the apply page nor the FAQ mentions a video requirement. I could not open the application form (it sits behind login) and I could not find a YC page with video advice. Past applications have asked for a short founder video. Check the live form before assuming either way.
- Requests for Startups (Fall 2026, 13 items). None mention simulation, synthetic users or forecasting. The closest is "Data for the Real World," which argues that once you can model a system you can control it and cites weather-balloon data feeding forecasts. "Multiplayer AI" (shared live agent sessions) loosely resembles our fork-and-ask-why surface. [YC RFS](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)
- Paul Graham's older essay on applying is still the best primary text I could open. Reviewers read about 100 applications a day. Put the point in the first sentence in the simplest terms. Avoid marketing language. Describe what you build plainly, with a familiar comparison if it helps. Founders matter more than the idea, so name one impressive thing each founder built. Disclose known flaws, because reviewers assume an unmentioned problem is one you have not considered. Edit ruthlessly. [PG, How to Apply to YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/howtoapply)
- Paul Graham on ideas: urgent need beats broad appeal, a small intense market that can grow is a good start, and a crowded market signals demand rather than danger. [PG, How to Get Startup Ideas](https://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html)
- Competitors named in the brief (Aaru, Simile, Jev/TypeSafe) were not researched here, so I cannot say whether YC has funded any of them. That is a gap, since partners will ask.

## What it means for Real World

- For the product: The 22-day plan is a plain fact to partners, so show what already runs. The strongest single artifact is the receipts page: forecasts published as hashes before outcomes exist, scored against market price. Even five or six scored days beats any slide. Do not ship ambitious surfaces that crowd this out. The simulate API and the receipts page matter more than the globe.
- For the engineering: Freeze a "demo path" by October 24: today's world loads, a fork takes one event, a 7-day ensemble runs, one agent answers why. Record the demo only from the live product. Keep the nightly job cheap and alive; a missed day on the public record is worse than a smaller cohort. Keep known failure modes (variance collapse, Western flattening) written down with measurements, because PG's advice is to disclose flaws before reviewers find them.
- For the business: The moat claim is the record that cannot be backfilled. That claim is only credible if the first hashed forecast predates the application by as long as possible, so publish the first hash this week, not on launch day. The $500K deal is standard, so nothing in our plan depends on negotiating it. A deep-tech framing (data assimilation, as in weather models) fits the "model it to control it" language in the RFS better than a "synthetic users for market research" framing, which is crowded.

## Recommendations

1. Publish the first hashed forecast this week and a public log of every nightly run. Owner: engineering. Done when the receipts page shows at least one pre-registered, hashed forecast with a timestamp.
2. Open the live YC application form now (log in, read every question, copy them into a doc) and confirm whether a video is required and its length. Owner: PM. Today.
3. Draft the answers by October 20 in PG's style: first sentence says what Real World is in plain words, with no market-size claims. Include one "impressive thing each founder built" line each.
4. Write a half-page "known flaws" section (variance collapse, Western flattening, contamination, cost of $15-60 a night, scope) with the mitigation and the measured result for each. Put the strongest honest version in the application.
5. Submit by October 30, not November 2. The deadline is 8:00 p.m. PT on a Monday and a late application is not marked on time.
6. Record the demo video from the live product, after October 27, one take, no slides, under about two minutes unless the form says otherwise. Show the fork, the ensemble, and the receipts page, in that order. Keep a silent backup recording in case the live product breaks.
7. Prepare a one-page competitor comparison (Aaru's missed 2024 election call, Simile, Jev/TypeSafe) using only public facts. Owner: PM.
8. Plan interviews now. They happen in November and December, so keep the nightly pipeline and the receipts page stable through the end of December.
