# Red team lens

- Date: 2026-10-11
- Seat: red team. Job: find the strongest reasons Real World fails, the places the notes contradict each other, and the claims nothing backs.
- Confidence: medium. I read 018, 022, 030, 059, 060, 089 and 092 in full and the rest through their summaries. I trust the research only as far as it goes, and many notes say their web search budget ran out, so several key facts rest on search snippets.

## Verdict

As written, the plan fails on three points, and none of them is engineering. First, the population cannot legally be built the way the pitch says: IPUMS International bans commercial use, and no attitude source we rely on (WVS, Gallup, Pew, the barometers) has confirmed commercial terms. Second, the headline metric probably cannot be won and cannot be measured by launch. We will have about 25 resolved questions against a rule-of-thumb floor of 100. LLMs tend to copy market prices. Our own assimilation feeds market moves into the forecast it then scores against those same markets. Third, "100,000 LLM agents simulating the whole world" describes something the research tells us not to build. The engine every note converges on is a few thousand segment-level distributions with a numeric belief model under them, and it may not beat one LLM call that says "describe the population". The company can survive this if it ships as what the evidence supports: a public, pre-committed, honestly losing-or-winning forecast ledger with a population model as one input, and with every licensing and accuracy claim cut back to what is true on Oct 27.

## Evaluation

**The data plan has no legal foundation.** IPUMS International forbids commercial use and redistribution, and every team member needs an individual licence (`data/046-ipums-international.md`, `data/060-data-licensing-legal.md`). Five other notes reach the same conclusion on their own (`science/026-population-synthesis.md`, `open-source/037-synthpop-tools.md`, `product/083-icp-games-media.md`, `business/095-partnerships.md`, `data/099-country-coverage-tiers.md`). Some notes still build on it anyway: `data/047-world-values-survey.md` joins WVS "to IPUMS agents", `data/048-gallup-pew-global.md` says "use IPUMS and WVS for the agent population", and `data/058-geodata.md` uses IPUMS GEOLEV boundaries without confirmed terms. The proposed escape, GLOPOP-S, is CC BY 4.0 on Dataverse, but the paper is NC-ND, it is built on LIS (non-commercial) and DHS inputs, and its base year is 2015 (`open-source/037-synthpop-tools.md`, `data/057-global-synthetic-population.md`). The attitude layer is worse: WVS terms sit in a PDF nobody read, Pew is excerpt-only, Gallup microdata is paid, and none of the barometers state commercial terms (`data/047`, `data/048`, `data/049-regional-barometers.md`). The two eval sets we would use to prove we are not Western-flattened, GlobalOpinionQA and SimBench, are CC BY-NC-SA (`science/020-opinion-benchmarks.md`, `science/027-cross-cultural-llm-bias.md`). Of the sources checked, GDELT is the only one clearly cleared for commercial use.

**The scoreboard cannot say anything by launch.** `data/059-resolvable-events-calendar.md` finds about 25 forecasts that can be registered in advance, and most of them resolve after Oct 27. `science/030-forecast-scoring.md` and `science/097-benchmark-design.md` both say to make no "beats the market" claim below roughly 100 resolved questions, and 030 admits even that figure is a rule of thumb. Our questions are serially correlated, which makes naive confidence intervals too narrow (030). So the YC application will show a ledger that is mostly unresolved. And where it does resolve, we should expect to lose: ForecastBench found LLMs largely copy market prices (`market/006-ai-forecasting-companies.md`), Douven found LLM ensembles still trail markets after removing contamination (`business/096-recent-news.md`), and the best result anywhere is GPT-5 roughly tying Kalshi (`science/021-llm-forecasting.md`).

**The assimilation design is circular.** The brief ingests "prediction-market moves" each morning and nudges beliefs so the world matches what it observes. Then it scores the forecast as Brier against market price. A forecast that has been assimilated toward the market converges on the market, so the best it can show is a gap of zero. `market/005-prediction-markets-landscape.md` sees the problem and asks for a blind run with no market prices, but `science/022-data-assimilation-abm.md` and `engineering/065-belief-update-operators.md` list market prices as observations. The plan contradicts itself, and nobody has decided which version gets scored.

**The 100,000 agents are marketing, not method.** Every engineering and science note backs away from per-agent LLM calls. The options they propose instead are archetypes (`science/028-large-scale-llm-sims.md`, `open-source/033-agenttorch.md`), distribution-first sampling (`science/019-calibration-methods.md`, `business/096`), Friedkin-Johnsen numeric updates with LLM calls for 1-2% of agents (`science/023-opinion-dynamics.md`), and an EnKF on segment cells (`science/022`, `open-source/038-data-assimilation-libs.md`). Meanwhile, independent persona agents collapse: twin SD is below human SD in 94% of 164 outcomes, and individual r is about 0.20 against 0.145 for demographics alone (`science/018-failure-modes.md`, `science/017-generative-agents.md`). A no-persona "describe the population" baseline beat persona panels on 9 of 9 pairs (`science/016-silicon-samples.md`), and no LLM beats a fitted non-LLM baseline at the individual level (`science/020`). Nobody has tested whether the swarm adds anything over one LLM reading the same news (`science/021`). That untested question is the whole thesis.

**"Whole world" means about 25 countries plus priors.** Rich data exists for about 20-30 mostly high-income countries (`data/099`). GDELT translates 65 languages, not the "100+" in the brief (`open-source/042-news-ingestion.md`, `data/050-gdelt.md`). Reuters reach data does not represent India, Kenya, Nigeria or South Africa (`science/025-news-exposure-diffusion.md`). China's latest IPUMS census is from 2000 (`data/046`). Cultural prompting makes 19-29% of countries worse (`science/027`). Claude is weak in low-resource languages (`engineering/073-multilingual-agents.md`). Unobserved regions drift to the model prior (`science/022`), and that prior is the Western default.

**The cost numbers disagree with each other and none were measured.** The nightly run is quoted as $17 (`engineering/061-system-architecture.md`), $7-25 (`engineering/062-llm-cost-choices.md`), $7-55 (`business/089-unit-economics.md`) and $9-18 per pass (`engineering/063-typed-probabilities.md`). `engineering/039-inference-structured-output.md` quotes about $8 per question. With 25 tracked questions, that is $100-200 a night, three times the brief's ceiling. A full fork is quoted as $185 (089), $350 (`market/013-api-pricing-benchmarks.md`) or $600 (061). Every one of these numbers assumes a token count. The daily human panel at $1.5-3k a day (`market/008-polling-economics.md`) is 25 to 200 times the LLM budget and is not in any plan.

**Reproducibility promises run into the API.** Haiku 5.5 has no logprobs and no seed, and it requires temperature 1 (`engineering/063`, `engineering/072-replay-and-caching.md`). "Replay" can only mean re-running our own engine on recorded outputs. The API promises a "seed" field (`engineering/069-api-design.md`), and that field must not imply the model is deterministic.

**The moat is thin, and the hash proves less than the pitch says.** An anchor proves the bytes have not changed since the anchor time. It does not prove the forecast was made first (`science/030`, `engineering/070-commit-reveal-timestamping.md`). A rival can start its own ledger tomorrow, and on launch day ours is 22 days old and statistically empty (`business/092-moat-theory.md`). If we choose which questions to publish after seeing how things go, we are backfilling by selection. Only a question list committed in advance closes that hole.

**The product is aimed at buyers who want something else.** The money is in brand and agency concept tests, not whole-world forecasts (`market/014-who-buys.md`, `product/080-icp-product-teams.md`). Finance needs point-in-time history we do not have (`market/015-alt-data-finance.md`). Games are out (`market/010-game-npc-ai.md`), campaigns are out (`product/082-icp-political.md`), and policy units pay little (`market/014`, `product/081-icp-policy-research.md`). Nobody pays for the globe.

**Launch week collides with our own safety rules.** The launch-week events are mostly elections. Israel votes on Oct 27, the day we launch (`data/059`). `engineering/074-abuse-and-safety.md` bans "election-result calls during voting", and `business/091-regulation.md` and `product/082` raise EU AI Act Annex III exposure. Nobody has squared the two.

**Backtests are banned in one note and ordered in another.** `science/030` and `science/097` forbid backtests because the model has seen the past. `science/022` asks for a 90-day hindcast, and `science/028` and `open-source/033` ask for ablations on "held-out" questions. Every pre-cutoff question is contaminated, so those results would not count.

**The research itself is thin in places.** Most notes record a spent search budget. Funhouse Mirrors is "opened" in `science/018` but "unverified" in `science/097` and `engineering/100-world-model-sota.md`. The Kalshi and Polymarket terms, which govern the headline metric, were never read (`market/005`, `data/051-prediction-market-apis.md`). The name is weak and may need changing (`product/085-naming-trademark.md`), and a rename after the first hash splits the record.

## Top 10 demands, in priority order

1. **Freeze the scored-question list before any forecasting.** Hash and anchor the list of questions, their resolution sources and the scoring rule first. Publish every committed forecast, losses included. Without this, "cannot be backfilled" is false (`science/030`, `science/097`, `engineering/070`).
2. **Score a blind forecast as the headline.** The forecast we score against markets must never see market prices, directly or through assimilation. The assimilated forecast can be a second, labelled row (`market/005`, `science/022`).
3. **Run a plain-LLM baseline every day next to the swarm, plus a non-LLM marginal baseline.** If by January the swarm does not beat one LLM call reading the same news, drop the agent claim from the pitch (`science/016`, `science/020`, `science/021`, `business/092`).
4. **Make licensing a launch gate.** Keep a SOURCES table in which "unknown" blocks a source from the shipped pipeline. No IPUMS anywhere, including geo boundaries. Send emails this week to IPUMS, WVS, Pew, the GLOPOP-S author, Kalshi and Polymarket. Until we have answers, label the population "calibrated to published marginals, research preview" (`data/060`, `data/046`, `data/057`, `data/099`).
5. **Do not display or redistribute market prices until someone has read the Kalshi and Polymarket terms in a browser.** Our headline metric depends on those terms (`data/051`, `market/005`).
6. **Stop saying "100,000 LLM agents" and "the whole world".** Say how many archetype calls we make. Publish the country tier on every output. Headline only Tier A and B countries (`science/028`, `data/099`, `science/027`).
7. **Build the cost ledger and measure 1,000 calls before quoting any dollar figure.** Count cost per tracked question, not per agent. Every spend path must fail closed when its cap is hit (`business/089`, `engineering/039`, `engineering/062`).
8. **Put the spread audit in the nightly run.** Compute the SD ratio against survey data for each country and item, and fail or flag any run below 0.7 (`science/018`, `science/028`).
9. **Settle the elections conflict by Oct 20.** Either aggregate election forecasts committed before polls open are allowed, or launch week runs on central-bank and data releases (`engineering/074`, `data/059`, `product/082`).
10. **Pick the name before the first hash** (`product/085-naming-trademark.md`).

## What to cut

- Opt-in twins. They raise GDPR Article 9 issues, cost about $90-100 per person, add about 12 points over demographics on one US sample, and are not validated outside the US (`science/017`, `business/091`, `business/092`).
- The daily human panel as a launch item. It costs $1.5-3k a day with no budget behind it. Replace it with daily-question answers, labelled "self-selected" (`market/008`).
- The neighbour-talk network and opinion dynamics in forks. Forks cannot be scored, so this machinery is unfalsifiable (`science/023`, `science/024`, `science/030`).
- Social media signals beyond GDELT (`data/055-social-media-data.md`).
- Native-language agent prompting (`engineering/073`).
- Stainless SDK polish, the MCP endpoint, and four post-launch "named launches". One API, one generated SDK.
- Enterprise, finance, policy, games and campaign sales motions before Nov 2.
- Any accuracy percentage, any "parity" claim, and any backtest number in public copy.

## Three numbers I will watch

1. **Swarm Brier minus plain-LLM Brier**, paired by question, with n and a day-block bootstrap interval. This one decides whether the agents are a product or a costume.
2. **Resolved, pre-committed questions (n).** Until n is about 100, every comparative claim is noise, and the pitch should say so.
3. **Data sources in the shipped pipeline with written commercial clearance, out of the total in use.** Anything under 100% on Oct 27 is a takedown risk on the very asset that is supposed to be the moat.
