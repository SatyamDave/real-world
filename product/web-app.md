# Web app

## What it does

One page that opens on one line of thesis, "Don't ask a model. Ask a world.", with the record as its proof ("Scored in public since <first day>: N days, M receipts", from `GET /v1/receipts`), then today's question, not a globe. The header says which world is shown ("World of <date>"). The visitor answers with a slider and sees the instant reveal, the streak and a 7-square grid of their week with a "Copy your week" button. Below it is a strip of the latest receipts and a "Fork today's world" box with three prefilled events and a free-text field; a fork shows progress day by day and ends in a card: one setup sentence, the top three segment shifts, one generated sentence of meaning ("Inflation worry rose 1 pt across 16 countries; the 5 runs agree within 1 pt."), each shift row with its "n = N agents", the band across ensemble members on an axis zoomed to that band's values, cost in cents ("Fake model, nothing spent" with the estimate at Haiku prices when no model ran; refused forks read "Not started: " and the API's `detail`), and three share actions: "Copy link" (reopens the same result), "Copy text" (event, sentence, disclosure, link) and "Save image" (a 1200x630 PNG drawn in the browser). On screens 768 px and wider, a globe behind everything shows each country shaded by the topic in view, with its coverage tier, and a "Read one agent: United States, Brazil, Kenya" row under the question opens the panel without finding a country on the globe. Below 768 px the three.js chunk is not loaded: the still globe poster shows at 240 px above the headline, as on the landing page, and a "Read one agent" list of every simulated country with its tier takes the live globe's place. Choosing a country opens one sampled agent with two clearly labelled panels, "What moved this agent" (data) and "In its own words" (simulated; its label adds "fake model, placeholder text" when no model ran). Each step shows the change in the agent's chance of leaning yes in percentage points, converted from the logit step the API returns, and traits read as words ("woman · 35-54 · higher education · urban"). A receipts page lists every committed forecast (the question's text with its id on hover, probabilities as percentages, commit times in local time with the UTC string on hover, and how many days before its resolution date it was committed) and verifies the hashes in the browser. Its score cards lead with "No claim yet" and the resolved n until 100 questions resolve, and show no interval below n = 10.

States, written out:

- Loading: "Loading <thing>" with a spinner, for every API call; the agent's account reads "Writing the account".
- Error: "Could not load <thing>. The server did not respond." (or "had an error", "refused the request", "It was not found."), with "Try again"; the response body is never shown.
- Empty: "No forecasts committed yet.", "No days committed yet.", "No forecast is hashed yet. The record starts with the first one.", "No segment of at least N agents moved." (N is the result's `top_shift_min_agents`)
- A fork id that does not exist: "This fork does not exist or has expired." Any other unknown path: "Page not found".

The look is the landing page's "Commit Slip" theme: the same colour tokens (cool paper, ink, verify green for committed, verified and anchored states, clay for pending), self-hosted IBM Plex Sans for reading and Plex Mono for data (dates, hashes, probabilities), one type scale (12, 14, 16, 20, 26, 40 px on a phone, a 64 px headline on desktop), one body colour, 44 px touch targets and a 2 px green focus ring. Today's question, the latest receipts, each day on the receipts page and the fork card are printed receipts: a header with a stamp ("Pending" in clay until the question resolves, "Resolved yes" or "Resolved no" in green after; "AI-simulated" on a fork), a torn bottom edge, and on the question a "Committed" column ("Shown after you answer", then the day's date linking its receipts; "Check the record" once answers close) and a "Resolves" column (the date and the resolution source's host). The fork's saved image is the same receipt, light in either theme. The landing work defines no dark theme, so dark mode keeps the same hues on a near-black green paper. On wide screens a still globe poster shows at once; the live globe loads after the page goes idle, fades in over it, and every simulated country rings once. The live globe is dot-sampled like the poster and the landing page's globe: simulated countries' dots are shaded by the topic in view, experimental ones are clay, and land that is not simulated is the line colour. With reduced motion the globe does not rotate or ring.

Every route sets its own title (a fork uses its event text) and ends in the landing page's footer: links to Receipts, Acceptable use, API (`/docs`) and Source (the GitHub repository), the line "Real World outputs are AI-simulated. Simulated populations are not polls and not predictions about real people." and "© 2026 Real World." No Privacy or Terms links until those pages are published. The page has a favicon, a static Open Graph image (`web/public/og.png`) and description, a skip link, and focus that moves into the agent panel on open and back on close.

## Who it is for

Curious visitors in their first 60 seconds, people opening a shared fork link, and skeptics checking receipts. It is also the PM's way to see that the whole system works.

## How we know it works

- Routes: `/` (daily question, receipts strip, fork box, globe), `/fork/{id}` (fork card, loads from `GET /v1/simulations/{id}`), `/receipts` (full list and verifier), `/acceptable-use` ([acceptable use](acceptable-use.md), word for word, in a receipt card), `/docs` (the API reference, rendered from `api/openapi.json` at build time: the curl first call, one receipt card per operation in the file's order with its summary, request body fields, response codes and refusal codes, the API key line, links to the API's raw `/openapi.json`, `/llms.txt` and interactive `/docs`, and the two SDK quickstarts read from their READMEs). The header links Today, Receipts and Docs.
- All data comes through `@real-world/sdk`; the app has no hand-written fetch calls.
- Component tests (Vitest) for: the reveal and week grid after submit, the closed state, error copy and retry, the fork card from a fixture result, the fork form showing an acceptable-use refusal (`Refused: <detail> See acceptable use.`, linking `/acceptable-use`) inline, the footer on every route, the acceptable-use page's rules and code table, the docs page listing every operation in `api/openapi.json` with its refusal codes, the empty shifts state, the missing-fork copy, the agent panel labels and share-point deltas, the receipts verifier passing on the shared test vector and failing on a changed byte, the score card's "No claim yet", the thesis and proof line, and the not-found route. `share.test.ts` covers the grid, the share texts, the logit-to-share conversion and the trait words.
- A Playwright run at 1440 and 375 px, light and dark, against the local API: no horizontal scroll, the globe chunk is not requested at 375 px, the fork image is 1200x630.
- `pnpm --filter web build` succeeds with no type errors; the bundle has no runtime errors on load against the local API (smoke test).
- A production build (`pnpm --filter web build`) first asks the API at `VITE_API_URL` for every question the app would show (the daily question and each receipt day's forecasts) and fails if any id starts with `fx-` or any resolution source is on `example.com`, or if the API cannot be reached. `vite build --mode fixtures` skips the check for a local smoke build on fixtures. `src/liveCheck.test.ts` covers the rule.
- Every result view shows "AI-simulated. Not a poll or a prediction of a real person" and the coverage tier ([messaging](../gtm/messaging.md) asks for "AI-simulated" on every output).
- `theme.test.ts`: every font file in `fonts.css` ships with `font-display: swap`, the OFL notice is present and `index.html` preloads the body face. Component tests cover the Pending and Resolved stamps, the Committed and Resolves columns, the green stamp on an anchored day, and that the globe waits for the load event and an idle moment.
- Largest contentful paint, measured with Playwright on a production build served with gzip: under 1.5 s on a warm desktop at 1440 px and under 2.5 s on a simulated phone (390 px, 4x CPU slowdown, 150 ms latency, 1.6 Mbps).
- Measured after launch: first-minute activation and share-to-visit rate ([research/product/077](../research/product/077-daily-question-mechanics.md)).

## What it is not

- Not 100,000 dots: countries are cells, one agent is fetched on click.
- No sign-in, accounts, settings, dashboard, dark-pattern prompts or onboarding popups.
- No server-rendered OG images in the base build: the page carries one static image, and a fork's image is drawn in the browser for the visitor to attach. Per-fork link previews come with hosting.
- No mobile app. The page works at phone width.
