\begin{abstract}
Simulated populations of agents whose beliefs are seeded from survey data and moved by news are increasingly used to study how societies respond to events, including populations driven by large language models (LLMs). They are run open loop: nothing corrects them when polls, prediction markets or search data arrive. Numerical weather prediction addressed the analogous problem with data assimilation. We formulate \emph{belief assimilation}: sequential state estimation in which the hidden state is the belief vector of each of about $10^5$ agents and every observation is an aggregate over many of them. The forecast model is a numeric anchored opinion-dynamics model whose anchors and news inputs are produced by LLMs. We propose a two-level scheme: a local ensemble transform Kalman filter updates segment-level belief moments and parameters, observation operators are evaluated on each member's full population, and a \emph{lift} maps the analysis back to agents. Under a single-factor ensemble structure with exact covariances, the exact per-agent Kalman increment is a loading-weighted segment shift plus an idiosyncratic term; our default lift differs from it by a term whose size relative to the shift is $\sigma_\zeta^2/(n_s\sigma_\eta^2)$ times the mismatch between loadings and operator weights, where $\sigma_\zeta^2/\sigma_\eta^2$ is the ratio of idiosyncratic to segment-level forecast variance and $n_s$ the segment size. With $M$ members, a per-agent filter instead adds independent noise of variance $\sigma_\zeta^2\,\tr\big((C_Y+R)^{-1}C_Y\big)/(M-1)$ to each agent per analysis. The default lift is therefore justified only in a stated window of $\sigma_\zeta/\sigma_\eta$, which the synthetic experiment measures. No implementation of this filter exists, so we report no results for it; two synthetic checks of the simpler operator the system runs today are summarized separately. We specify three evaluations, a synthetic-truth study, a real-data hindcast and a live forecast record scored against market prices, with five primary hypotheses under graph-based family-wise error control. The primaries test skill against a free run, preservation of within-segment heterogeneity, hindcast skill, the value of agents over two segment-level filters (one of them the system's filter), and non-inferiority to market prices; comparison with poll aggregators and robustness to model misspecification are secondary. The market comparison will cover few countries and is likely to be underpowered within its time limit. The protocol file will be hashed and published before the first run.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

LLM agents conditioned on demographic backstories can reproduce some aggregate survey patterns~\cite{argyle2023}, and agents grounded in interviews track individual respondents better than demographics alone~\cite{park2024}; and a recent position paper argues that such simulations are a promising research method once several tractable challenges are addressed~\cite{anthis2025}. They have motivated simulations of societies ranging from tens of agents to millions~\cite{park2023,piao2025,yang2024oasis,chopra2024}; the largest replace a model call per agent with a small set of LLM ``archetypes''~\cite{chopra2024}. The same literature documents why such simulations go wrong when left alone. LLM respondents vary less than real survey respondents~\cite{bisbee2024}, pile onto modal answers~\cite{ozkan2026}, flatten identity groups~\cite{wang2024flatten}, and as digital twins show insufficient individuation and stereotyping~\cite{peng2025funhouse}. Their answers to cross-national opinion questions sit closer to some countries than others~\cite{durmus2023}, in one study closest to English-speaking and Protestant European societies~\cite{tao2024}; their answers are sensitive to prompt wording and change over time as the underlying model is updated~\cite{bisbee2024}; and networks of LLM agents converge toward the model's view of what is true rather than toward realistic disagreement~\cite{chuang2023}. A simulated world started from a prior and run forward will diverge from the real one, and nothing in the simulation says how far.

Weather forecasting faced a similar structure: a high-dimensional model with systematic error, and sparse, noisy, indirect observations every day. Its answer was the forecast--analysis cycle of data assimilation (DA)~\cite{carrassi2018}. Each cycle forecasts the state, compares the forecast with observations through an explicit observation operator, and corrects the state in proportion to the relative uncertainty of forecast and data. Ensemble methods, starting with the ensemble Kalman filter (EnKF)~\cite{evensen1994,burgers1998,houtekamer1998}, make this tractable for models with millions of state variables and ensembles of tens to a few hundred members~\cite{houtekamer2016}. The analogy is weaker than it looks: belief dynamics have no governing physical law, so model error is larger and less structured.

This paper asks what DA looks like when the state is the belief of every agent in a large synthetic population. Three features distinguish it from geophysics and from prior DA for agent-based models (ABMs)~\cite{ward2016,kieu2020,malleson2020,makinoshima2022,oswald2024,suchak2024}. (i) \emph{Nothing observes individuals.} Every observation is an aggregate over many agents, so the individual-level posterior is barely identified, and when the idiosyncratic part of the forecast spread is large enough (a condition Proposition~\ref{prop:noise} states and E1 measures) a per-agent ensemble update mostly adds sampling noise. (ii) \emph{Heterogeneity is the point.} The reason to simulate agents rather than aggregates is to keep differences between people, so an update that adds spurious differences or erases real ones destroys what LLM-seeded populations already represent poorly. (iii) \emph{The observations differ in kind.} A poll measures a stated response to a worded question, a market price measures traders' forecast of an outcome, and a news story measures what an outlet chose to write. Treating all three as noisy readings of ``belief'' is a category error.

\paragraph{Scope.} The filter in this paper runs on a numeric anchored opinion-dynamics model (Equation~\eqref{eq:fj}). LLMs set each agent's anchor and turn each news story into a numeric signal; they are not called per agent per day. This is a design decision: the ensemble runs on a cheap model, and increments are applied to any LLM agents afterwards. We specify that write-back interface (\S\ref{sec:setup}) and use per-agent LLM simulation only as a misspecified truth in one synthetic experiment. Claims below are about the numeric model. The design in~\cite{rw_askaworld} specifies a simpler v1 filter: a per-segment iterated extended Kalman filter on polls, with no cross-segment covariance, no social graph and no market observations. The engine built so far runs a simpler operator still: a scalar Kalman update of each country's mean belief logit per topic, with an anchor offset and variance inflation from the innovation, which replaced capped nudging after a synthetic check (\S\ref{sec:prior}). This paper is the upgrade path for both. Whichever is the public filter when the protocol file is frozen is a baseline in every experiment (B7), and the upgrade is adopted, as a new and separately hashed version, only if it beats B7 out of sample (H10, \S\ref{sec:e2}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Formulation.} Belief assimilation as filtering in a state-space model whose state is a population of agent belief vectors with fixed individual anchors and whose observations are aggregate functionals, with explicit identifying constraints for the latent scale, source biases and search salience (\S\ref{sec:setup}, \S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item \textbf{Algorithm.} A two-level ensemble filter: a localized ensemble transform Kalman filter over segment moments and augmented parameters, observation operators on full populations (asynchronous for polls), a lift back to agents, and a regime mixture over discrete scenarios (\S\ref{sec:method}, Figure~\ref{fig:cycle}).
  \item \textbf{Analysis of lifts.} The lifts are design choices, each the minimizer of a stated change metric. Using standard Kalman algebra on a factor model, we characterize the gap between the default lift and the exact per-agent increment, give the variance a finite-ensemble per-agent update adds for several correlated observations and its accumulation over days, and derive the window of forecast-spread structure in which the default lift is justified (Propositions~\ref{prop:lift}--\ref{prop:noise}). Each becomes a measured quantity or a test.
  \item \textbf{Observation operators.} Operators and error models for polls (fieldwork-averaged), belief-determined prediction markets (a per-member projection under the forecast model's mean reversion) and search interest, and news as forcing with poll and market reports removed (\S\ref{sec:obs}).
  \item \textbf{Evaluation protocol.} Hypotheses, baselines including the system's current filter, a per-agent filter at full size, a segment-only model and dynamic multilevel regression and post-stratification, metrics, graph-based multiplicity control, tuning budgets, predictive distributions, power rules, and every quantity still to be fixed with the rule that fixes it (\S\ref{sec:protocol}, Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}).
\end{enumerate}

This paper is one of a set. The end-to-end system and its comparison against asking a model directly are in~\cite{rw_askaworld}; this paper owns the filter, its ablations and the synthetic-truth experiments. Agents, anchors and segments come from~\cite{rw_unequally}, response-level correction of variance collapse from~\cite{rw_variance}, forecast commitment and scoring at call time from~\cite{rw_receipts}, the question set from~\cite{rw_bench}, the evidence on silicon-sample validity from~\cite{rw_sok}, and governance of public release from~\cite{rw_governing}. Appendix~\ref{app:inputs} states what this paper needs from them, so it can be read alone.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Ensemble data assimilation.} The EnKF~\cite{evensen1994,evensen2003} replaces the Kalman filter's covariance with an ensemble estimate; the stochastic version perturbs observations~\cite{burgers1998,houtekamer1998}, and square-root variants update deterministically~\cite{anderson2001,bishop2001}. Local ensemble filters compute the analysis independently per region~\cite{ott2004}; the local ensemble transform Kalman filter (LETKF)~\cite{hunt2007} does so in ensemble space and localizes by multiplying the inverse observation-error covariance by a factor that decays with distance. This observation-space (R) localization behaves differently from localizing the background covariance (B localization)~\cite{greybush2011}. Observations at times other than the analysis time can be assimilated with the ensemble's own cross-time covariances~\cite{hunt2004}. Small ensembles underestimate spread and create spurious long-range correlations; covariance inflation~\cite{anderson1999,whitaker2012} and localization with compactly supported tapers~\cite{gaspari1999} are standard remedies, and localization can be estimated from the ensemble when no physical distance is available~\cite{andersonlei2013}. Parameters can be estimated by state augmentation~\cite{evensen2009}; \citet{ruiz2013} review which parameters ensemble methods can and cannot identify this way. Variables with bounded or skewed distributions can be assimilated after a transformation to near-Gaussian form~\cite{bertino2003}. Kalman filters on a reduced state space have long been used when the full state is too large to filter~\cite{cane1996}. Particle filters~\cite{gordon1993} make no Gaussian assumption but need ensembles that grow exponentially with the effective dimension of the observations~\cite{snyder2008,vanleeuwen2019}; Rao--Blackwellised filters sample some variables and integrate the rest analytically~\cite{doucet2000}, the interacting multiple model filter runs one Kalman filter per regime of a Markov-switching system and mixes them by their likelihoods~\cite{blom1988}, and Gaussian-mixture ensemble filters bridge the EnKF and particle filters~\cite{stordal2011}. Nudging relaxes the model toward observations with a fixed gain~\cite{hoke1976}. Outside geophysics, ensemble filters on compartmental and metapopulation epidemic models assimilate aggregate incidence~\cite{shaman2012,pei2018}.

\paragraph{DA and inference for agent-based and opinion models.} An EnKF calibrated a pedestrian footfall ABM, working with log-transformed counts~\cite{ward2016}; parameter calibration and DA have been combined for a bus-route ABM~\cite{kieu2020}; and an EnKF has been coupled to an agent-based crowd model of a train station, updating agents' states in real time~\cite{suchak2024}. A bootstrap particle filter over the joint positions of all pedestrians needed a particle count that the authors found grows exponentially with the number of agents~\cite{malleson2020}. Treating each particle as a whole simulation with different latent parameters, and observing only aggregate density, scaled to thousands of people~\cite{makinoshima2022}; a particle filter improved a country-level policy-diffusion ABM~\cite{oswald2024}. Sequential Monte Carlo has estimated ABM parameters in economics~\cite{lux2018}, and neural simulation-based inference fits static parameters~\cite{cranmer2020,dignum2025}. Closest to us on the opinion side, \citet{kolic2025} compare an EnKF with likelihood-based inference for latent per-agent opinions in a bounded-confidence model and find DA competitive for aggregates but weaker for individual opinions; \citet{monti2020} learn opinion-dynamics parameters and latent opinions from social traces; and \citet{hendrickx2021} assimilate polls and Twitter opinion for the Brexit referendum, with no agent population. We know of no work that assimilates aggregate-only observations into a population of $10^5$ agents with LLM-derived inputs while constraining what the update does to within-group heterogeneity.

\paragraph{LLM agent populations.} Silicon samples~\cite{argyle2023}, generative agents~\cite{park2023,park2024} and large LLM-driven societies~\cite{piao2025,yang2024oasis,chopra2024} are open-loop simulators. Evaluations report distortions in means and variances~\cite{santurkar2023,durmus2023,bisbee2024,wang2024flatten,meister2024,ozkan2026}. DeGroot averaging~\cite{degroot1974}, Friedkin--Johnsen anchoring~\cite{friedkin1990} and bounded confidence~\cite{hegselmann2002} supply numeric update rules; networks of LLM agents deviate from them toward consensus on the model's view of the truth~\cite{chuang2023}. We do not try to make the open-loop simulator correct; we correct its state with data each day.

\paragraph{Poll aggregation, small-area opinion and forecasting.} State-space aggregators treat opinion as a latent random walk observed through polls with sampling error; \citet{jackman2005} adds pollster house effects identified by a constraint, \citet{linzer2013} gives a dynamic Bayesian state model across states, and \citet{heidemanns2020} update that approach for a national forecast. Multilevel regression and post-stratification (MRP) estimates subgroup and small-area opinion from sparse polls~\cite{park2004mrp}, including deep interactions among small subgroups~\cite{ghitza2013}, and has been run over a campaign~\cite{wang2015xbox}; it is the direct competitor to our segment means. Poll error exceeds sampling error by a substantial nonsampling component~\cite{shiranimehr2018}. Prediction market prices are informative aggregate forecasts~\cite{wolfers2004}; whether they beat polls depends on how each is debiased: polls projected to election day outperformed vote-share markets in \citet{erikson2008}, while debiased markets beat debiased polls early in the cycle and in uncertain races in \citet{rothschild2009}. LLM forecasters have been compared with different references: a retrieval system nears the crowd aggregate of competitive forecasters and in some settings surpasses it~\cite{halawi2024}; on ForecastBench, expert forecasters outperform the best LLM~\cite{karger2024}; in Prophet Arena LLMs aggregate information more slowly than markets as resolution nears~\cite{yang2025prophet}; and LLMs remained substantially less accurate than prediction markets even when the market was evaluated at each model's training cutoff~\cite{douven2026}. GDELT provides machine-coded news events and tone~\cite{leetaru2013}, and search volume has nowcast economic and epidemic quantities~\cite{choi2012,ginsberg2009}, with failures when the mapping from search to target drifts~\cite{lazer2014}.

\section{Problem setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Population and anchors.} A population has agents $i=1,\dots,N$ ($N\approx10^5$), allocated across countries as in~\cite{rw_unequally}: a floor of 100 per country and the rest in proportion to population (Appendix~\ref{app:inputs} gives the formula; most countries hold a few hundred agents and only the most populous reach $10^3$). Each agent has covariates $z_i$ (country, age, sex, education, settlement type and a survey-derived attitude cluster) and a segment label $g(i)\in\{1,\dots,S\}$, a cell of a coarse partition of covariate space within a country. The v1 lattice of~\cite{rw_askaworld} (country $\times$ three age bands $\times$ three education levels, merged when small) has up to about 1,800 segments averaging about 55 agents. Our running example and E1's main arm use a stylized configuration with $S=500$ segments of about $n_s\approx200$ agents; real allocations have many smaller segments, down to the 50-agent merge floor, and E1 has an arm with that distribution. Each agent carries a fixed \emph{anchor} $a_i\in\R^K$ over $K\approx20$ propositions and a susceptibility $w_i\in(0,1]$, produced by population synthesis~\cite{rw_unequally} (Appendix~\ref{app:inputs}) and never overwritten by assimilation. The demographic skeleton is drawn from GLOPOP-S~\cite{ton2024glopop,ton2024glopopdata}, not from census microdata whose terms bar commercial use (\S\ref{sec:ethics}).

\paragraph{Belief state and its scale.} The mutable state is $x_{i,t}\in\R^K$. Coordinate $x_{i,k,t}$ is a latent position on topic $k$, in the units of the log-odds that agent $i$ would endorse a fixed \emph{reference item} for that topic. Other items reach this scale through item maps (\S\ref{sec:obs}). Log-odds remove the boundary at $0$ and $1$ and make additive Gaussian errors less unreasonable, as Gaussian anamorphosis does for bounded variables in ensemble DA~\cite{bertino2003}. We write $X_t\in\R^{N\times K}$ for the population state.

\paragraph{Forecast model.} The state evolves under a simulator $X_{t+1} = \mathcal{M}(X_t, F_t, \theta, \xi_t)$, where $F_t$ is exogenous news forcing, $\theta$ are global behavioural parameters and $\xi_t$ is model noise. Every result in this paper uses the Friedkin--Johnsen-type instance
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
x_{i,t+1} = x_{i,t} &+ w_i\Big[\lambda\, \chi_{c(i)}E_i F_t + \gamma \sum\nolimits_j A_{ij}(x_{j,t}-x_{i,t})\Big]\\
&+ \rho\big(a_i + b_{g(i)} - x_{i,t}\big) + \xi_{i,t},
\end{split}
\label{eq:fj}
\end{equation}
where $E_i$ is agent $i$'s exposure to each news signal (from language, country and media diet), $\chi_{c(i)}$ is the latent search salience of each topic in agent $i$'s country (\S\ref{sec:obs}), $A$ is a sparse social graph, $\theta=(\lambda,\gamma,\rho)$ are the news gain, peer coupling and anchor pull, $b_s\in\R^K$ is a segment-level \emph{anchor offset}, zero-mean in the prior and estimated by the filter, and $\xi_{i,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_\xi^2 I_K)$ independently across agents and days. The noise scale $\sigma_\xi$ is not augmented: to first order the ensemble covariance between a noise scale and the predicted observations is zero, so an ensemble filter cannot learn it. It is chosen on the tuning split from a grid so that the mean squared innovation matches its predicted value $s_Y^2+r$ (\S\ref{sec:conventions}). Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} costs $O(NK+\mathrm{nnz}(A)K)$ per step and no tokens. Without forcing it is mean-reverting: each agent relaxes toward $a_i+b_{g(i)}$ at rate $\rho$. The v1 public build has no social graph ($\gamma=0$); we keep the coupling term because the filter must handle it if a graph is added. The v1 forecast model of~\cite{rw_askaworld} is the special case with no coupling, equal susceptibility, exposure and noise within a segment, and agents initialized at their anchors: every agent then moves by its segment's shift, and the loading-shift lift below with $\ell_i=1$ is exact. Because Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} is linear in $x$, segment means follow the average of agent dynamics exactly when $\gamma=0$; what an agent population adds over a segment-level model is the distribution of beliefs within segments, which enters through nonlinear observation operators and second moments (B6, H10).

\paragraph{Where LLMs enter.} (1) Anchors $a_i$ are produced from covariates and survey aggregates by the companion pipeline. (2) Each news story is read once by an LLM into a signed signal on the topics it touches, shared across agents, giving $\hat F_t$ (\S\ref{sec:obs}). (3) Optionally, a \emph{display world} of LLM agents can be kept in step with the filter. Its interface is: each LLM agent stores $x_{i,k}$ as a number; the read-out is that stored number; each day the agent is prompted with its profile, its stored position rendered as a stance on a seven-point scale, and the day's stories, and returns a seven-point stance mapped back to log-odds by a fixed table; after each analysis the lifted value overwrites the stored number. The display world is a single world, not an ensemble member, so its cost is one LLM call per agent per day and the ensemble never runs LLM agents. Its noise model is the seven-point quantization plus response variability measured by repeated calls. We use it only as the truth generator in the fraternal twin, where it runs \emph{without} write-back, and in an exploratory coherence check (\S\ref{sec:e1}); E2 and E3 run Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} alone.

\paragraph{Observations.} On day $t$ we observe a vector $y_t$ of aggregates, each with operator $h_o$ and error variance $r_o$, $y_{o,t} = h_o(X;\beta) + \epsilon_{o,t}$ with $\epsilon_{o,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,r_{o,t})$, where $\beta$ are source-level biases (house effects, market intercepts, search offsets) and $h_o$ may depend on past states (polls are fielded before they are released). Every $h_o$ depends on the population only through weighted population or subpopulation averages of functions of individual beliefs. This is the defining constraint of the problem.

\paragraph{Goal.} Given $y_{1:t}$ and $F_{1:t}$, produce an ensemble $\{X_t^{(m)},\theta^{(m)},\beta^{(m)},b^{(m)},\chi^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^M$ approximating the filtering distribution, forecast seven days ahead, and publish probabilities for belief-determined questions before their outcomes exist.

\paragraph{Why not filter every agent.} A per-agent ensemble filter updates each agent by \[\Delta x_i = \widehat{\Cov}(x_i, y)\,\big(C_Y+R\big)^{-1}(y-\bar y),\] with $C_Y$ the sample covariance of the predicted observations over $M\approx50$ members. If member perturbations within a segment have a segment-level component $\ell_i\eta$ and an idiosyncratic component $\zeta_i$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:factor} below), then $\Cov(x_i,y)$ is of the same order as $\Cov(\mu,y)$ for the segment mean $\mu$, so the true per-agent gain is not small. The difficulty is the sample covariance between each agent's idiosyncratic part and $y$: it is noise of order $1/\sqrt{M-1}$, independent across agents, so every agent receives a random increment unrelated to the data. Proposition~\ref{prop:noise} gives its variance, $\sigma_\zeta^2\tr\big((C_Y+R)^{-1}C_Y\big)/(M-1)$ per analysis, which is $k\sigma_\zeta^2/(M-1)$ for a single observation that removes a fraction $k$ of forecast variance. For a single observation this noise exceeds the signal in agent $i$'s gain when $\sigma_\zeta/\sigma_\eta>\ell_i\,|\mathrm{corr}(\eta,y)|\sqrt{M-1}$. For a poll of one segment $|\mathrm{corr}|\approx1$ and the threshold is about $7\ell_i$ at $M=50$; for a national poll that aggregates $S_c$ roughly independent segments $|\mathrm{corr}|\approx S_c^{-1/2}$, and the threshold falls by about $\sqrt{S_c}$. Whether the condition holds is a property of the forecast ensemble, which E1 measures. Unlike geophysics, no spatial distance separates agents within a segment, so distance-based localization does not remove this noise. The structured update of \S\ref{sec:lift} does not use per-agent sample covariances: it applies the segment-mean increment through loadings estimated by pooling all agents of a segment, so this per-agent sampling noise does not enter it. It still changes within-segment spread through unequal loadings (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank}(a)), and E1 reports that change. The same mechanism appears one level up. Within a country block there is no distance between segments, so a national poll updates the $S_c$ segment means of that country through 50-member sample covariances, with sampling noise relative to signal of about $\sqrt{S_c/(M-1)}$. In large countries with many segments this is not small. H2 protects only within-segment heterogeneity, so between-segment structure within a country is tested separately (H14). A per-agent particle filter fails faster~\cite{snyder2008,malleson2020}.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Reduced state}
For each member we define a reduced state from the full population,
\begin{equation}
v = \Phi(X,\theta,\beta,b,\chi) = \big[\,\mu_{s,k},\ b_{s,k}\ \ (s\le S,\ k\le K);\ \ \log \chi_{c,k};\ \ \theta;\ \beta\,\big],
\end{equation}
where $\mu_{s,k}$ is the mean of $x_{i,k}$ over segment $s$ and $\log \chi_{c,k}$ the log search salience per country and topic. With $S=500$ and $K\approx20$, $\dim v$ is about $2\times10^4$ plus the salience, parameter and bias entries, split into local blocks of a few hundred. In A2, which uses the moment lift where ordinal data constrain spread, $v$ also contains $\log\sigma_{s,k}$, the log within-segment standard deviation. A1 omits it: under A1's lift an analysed spread would not be imposed on agents, so it would be dead state.

\subsection{Initial ensemble and model error}
\label{sec:init}
Members share covariates $z_i$ and the graph $A$. They differ in everything population synthesis is uncertain about: $b_s^{(m)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{b,\tau}^2)$ with a larger prior standard deviation in lower population tiers $\tau$ (Appendix~\ref{app:inputs}), so segment-level synthesis bias such as the skew toward Western opinion is inside the ensemble; individual anchor noise $a_i^{(m)}=a_i+\nu_i^{(m)}$, $\nu_i^{(m)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_\nu^2)$; susceptibilities $w_i^{(m)}=\min(1,w_i e^{\varepsilon_i^{(m)}})$, $\varepsilon_i^{(m)}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{w}^2)$; and $\theta^{(m)},\beta^{(m)},\chi^{(m)}$ drawn from their priors. The scales $\sigma_{b,\tau}$, $\sigma_\nu$ and $\sigma_w$ are not tuned by any method. They are fixed before E1 by an external criterion, the validation error of the anchor pipeline of~\cite{rw_unequally} on held-out survey respondents (Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}), because $\sigma_\nu$ in particular sets a permanent floor on the idiosyncratic spread $\sigma_\zeta$ and would otherwise be a free knob that decides the comparison between per-agent and structured filters. Each member starts at $x_{i,0}^{(m)}=a_i^{(m)}+b^{(m)}_{g(i)}$ and cycles for a 30-day spin-up before any score is recorded. Parameters follow random walks with per-day standard deviation $c$ times their prior standard deviation, with $c\in\{0.01,0.03,0.1\}$ chosen per parameter group ($\theta$, $\beta$, $b$, $\log\chi$) during tuning (\S\ref{sec:conventions}). $M=50$ in E2 and E3; E1 sweeps $M$.

\medskip
\noindent\fbox{\parbox{0.97\linewidth}{
\textbf{Algorithm 1: one belief-assimilation cycle (day $t$).}\\[2pt]
\textbf{Input:} analysis ensemble $\{X^{(m)}_{t-1},\theta^{(m)},\beta^{(m)},b^{(m)},\chi^{(m)}\}_{m=1}^{M}$ (grouped into regime sub-ensembles if regimes are used) and each member's stored daily populations for the last 14 days; story signals $\hat F_t$ with reading spread $\Sigma_F$; observations $y_t$ with variances $r_t$.
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \emph{Forecast.} For each $m$: draw $F^{(m)}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\hat F_t,\Sigma_F)$; set $X^{(m)}_t=\mathcal{M}(X^{(m)}_{t-1},F^{(m)}_t,\theta^{(m)},\xi^{(m)}_t)$; random-walk $\theta^{(m)},\beta^{(m)},b^{(m)},\log \chi^{(m)}$; store $X^{(m)}_t$.
\item \emph{Predicted observations.} $Y^{(m)} = h(X^{(m)};\beta^{(m)})$, computed on all $N$ agents, on stored past days for polls (\S\ref{sec:obs}).
\item \emph{Regime weights (optional).} Update regime probabilities from the forecast predictive likelihood (\S\ref{sec:pf}).
\item \emph{Reduce.} $v^{(m)}=\Phi(X^{(m)}_t,\theta^{(m)},\beta^{(m)},b^{(m)},\chi^{(m)})$.
\item \emph{Local ETKF.} For each block $\ell$ (one country $\times$ topic group), select observations within the localization radius, taper their errors with a Gaspari--Cohn weight~\cite{gaspari1999}, and update $v_\ell$ by Equation~\eqref{eq:letkf} with prior multiplicative inflation $\alpha$. Average block analyses of $\theta$ (\S\ref{sec:params}).
\item \emph{Relax.} Apply relaxation to prior spread~\cite{whitaker2012} with factor $\phi$ to the analysis perturbations of $v$.
\item \emph{Lift.} $X^{a(m)}_t = L(X^{(m)}_t, v^{a(m)})$ (\S\ref{sec:lift}); set $\theta^{(m)},\beta^{(m)},b^{(m)},\chi^{(m)}$ to their analysis values. The population $X^a$ is the carried state; $v$ is recomputed from it next cycle.
\item \emph{Forecast and commit.} Run each member to $t+7$; compute probabilities for belief-determined questions (\S\ref{sec:obs}, ``Question probabilities''); commit their hashes before resolution~\cite{rw_receipts}.
\end{enumerate}
}}
\medskip

\paragraph{Local ETKF update.} Following \citet{hunt2007}, let $V\in\R^{d\times M}$ hold the forecast perturbations $v^{(m)}-\bar v$ for block $\ell$, $Y\in\R^{p\times M}$ the predicted-observation perturbations for its $p$ local observations, and $\tilde R$ the tapered observation error covariance. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\tilde P = \Big[\tfrac{M-1}{\alpha} I + Y^\top \tilde R^{-1} Y\Big]^{-1},\qquad
\bar w = \tilde P\, Y^\top \tilde R^{-1}(y-\bar Y),\\
W = \big[(M-1)\tilde P\big]^{1/2},\qquad
v^{a(m)} = \bar v + V(\bar w + W_{:,m}).
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:letkf}
\end{equation}
The matrices inverted are $M\times M$. The inflations act in a fixed order: $\alpha$ inflates the prior inside Equation~\eqref{eq:letkf}, then relaxation to prior spread acts on the analysis perturbations before the lift. $\alpha$ and $\phi$ are tuned jointly on a grid.

\paragraph{Localization without space.} The distance between a state block (country $c$, topic $k$) and an observation (population $c'$, item $k'$) is $d = d_{\mathrm{geo}}(c,c') + d_{\mathrm{top}}(k,k')$: $d_{\mathrm{geo}}$ is zero within a country and is a shortest-path length on an a-priori graph of shared language, media and migration ties, and $d_{\mathrm{top}}$ is one minus a fixed topic similarity from item wording. The LETKF localizes by multiplying the inverse observation-error covariance of each block by a factor that decays with distance~\cite{hunt2007}, which is R localization~\cite{greybush2011}. Our own inference from that construction is that $d$ need not be a metric: each block's taper is applied to its own observations, and no localization matrix over state variables, positive definite or otherwise, is formed. The radius grid is in Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}. Localization encodes a prior that a poll in one country should not move beliefs in an unrelated country through a correlation that exists only in a 50-member sample. It does nothing within a country, where segments are at distance zero (see ``Why not filter every agent''). Empirical localization~\cite{andersonlei2013} is a sensitivity variant.

\subsection{Parameters, offsets and identifiability}
\label{sec:params}
Augmenting the state with $\theta$, $\beta$, $b$ and $\log\chi$~\cite{evensen2009,ruiz2013} lets the filter learn the news gain, coupling, anchor pull, source biases and salience. Each local block carries its own copy of $\theta$; after the block analyses, member $m$'s global $\theta^{a(m)}$ is the average of the block analyses weighted by each block's observational information $\tr(Y_\ell^\top\tilde R_\ell^{-1}Y_\ell)$. This average is not a posterior, so its spread is not a calibrated uncertainty; E1 reports the coverage of $\theta$ intervals (H6), and a separate global update using all observations is a sensitivity variant. House effects, market intercepts and search offsets belong to the block of their country and topic. The anchor offset matters for a reason specific to anchored models: without it, the pull $\rho(a_i-x_i)$ undoes each analysis increment within about $1/\rho$ days, so a persistent gap between simulated and real segments would be corrected every morning and reopened every night. With $b_s$ in the state, a persistent gap is absorbed as a shift of where the segment reverts to, while $a_i$, and with it each agent's position relative to its segment, is untouched. Identification rests on five constraints, stated here because aggregate data alone cannot separate additive terms:
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item Each topic's reference item has fixed $\kappa=1$, $c=0$. Every other item's map $(\kappa_o,c_o)$ is fitted once by linking its marginals to the reference item on survey waves that predate the evaluation window, and is then fixed and never augmented, so it cannot trade off against $\mu_s$ or $b_s$.
\item House effects sum to zero across pollsters within each country and item family, enforced by subtracting their mean from each member after every analysis. Where an election resolves a quantity a poll measures, the election result replaces this constraint as the anchor, which is how \citet{jackman2005} identifies house effects.
\item $b_s$ and $\mu_s$ are separated only by time scale: $\mu_s$ responds to forcing and reverts at rate $\rho$, while $b_s$ is a slow random walk with prior standard deviation $\sigma_{b,\tau}$. E1 reports the posterior correlation of $b$ and $\mu$ and the long-run path of $b$ as diagnostics.
\item Market slopes are fixed at one, market intercepts share a zero-mean prior whose small variance is set in Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}, and the market operator uses a fixed anchor pull $\bar\rho$ rather than each member's $\rho^{(m)}$, so a price can be fitted neither by its own bias term nor by changing $\rho$ instead of beliefs (\S\ref{sec:obs}).
\item Search salience is normalized to geometric mean one per series over the pre-window period, with the search offset $\beta_{c,k,t}$ absorbing the level, and the offset's random-walk step is a tenth of the salience's, so fast changes are attributed to salience and slow drift to the offset. E1 reports their posterior correlation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Lifting the analysis to agents}
\label{sec:lift}
The analysis gives segment means $\mu^a_{s,k}$ (and, in A2, spreads $\sigma^a_{s,k}$); agents must be moved to agree with them. Fix a segment with $n$ agents and one topic, write $x_i$ for a member's forecast beliefs with mean $\mu$ and standard deviation $\sigma$, and $\delta=\mu^a-\mu$.

\paragraph{Loadings.} Under Equation~\eqref{eq:fj}, member-to-member perturbations do not load equally on agents. A difference in news gain or forcing enters agent $i$ as $w_iE_iF$ accumulated with decay $(1-\rho)$; a difference in anchor pull enters as $a_i+b-x_i$; a difference in $b_s$ enters every agent equally through $\rho$; a difference in coupling depends on $i$'s neighbours. We therefore model the forecast ensemble within a segment as
\begin{equation}
x_i^{(m)} = \tilde x_i + \ell_i\,\eta^{(m)} + \zeta_i^{(m)},
\label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}
with a segment factor $\eta$ (variance $\sigma_\eta^2$), loadings $\ell_i>0$ normalized to $\bar\ell=1$, and idiosyncratic $\zeta_i$ (variance $\sigma_\zeta^2$) independent of each other and of $\eta$. The loadings are a low-dimensional function $\ell_i=\varphi^\top \psi_i$ of covariates $\psi_i=(1,\ w_i\tilde E_{i},\ a_i+b_{g(i)}-\bar x_i)$, evaluated at the ensemble-mean state $\bar x_i$ so that all members share them, where $\tilde E_i$ is agent $i$'s exposure to the topic accumulated with decay $(1-\rho)$. The three coefficients $\varphi$ are estimated per segment by least squares of $x_i^{(m)}-\bar x_i$ on $\psi_i(\mu^{(m)}-\bar\mu)$, pooled over all $nM$ agent--member pairs. Under Equation~\eqref{eq:factor} the ordinary least-squares standard error of each coefficient is of order $\sigma_\zeta/(\sigma_\eta\,\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{rms}}\sqrt{nM})$; E1 reports a bootstrap over members. Estimated loadings are floored at $0.05$ and then rescaled to mean one, so Proposition~\ref{prop:lift}(a)'s normalization holds; E1 reports sensitivity to floors of $0.01$ and $0.2$. Coupling-driven and other multi-factor structure is left in $\zeta$; that misfit is what H7 measures.

\begin{definition}[Lifts]
The \emph{ensemble-weight lift} $L_0$ applies the block's LETKF transform to each agent: $x_i^{a(m)}=\bar x_i+\sum_j (x_i^{(j)}-\bar x_i)(\bar w + W_{:,m})_j$, which is the per-agent ETKF with block localization and costs $O(NKM^2)$. The \emph{loading-shift lift} is $L_1:\ x_i' = x_i + \ell_i\,\delta$. The \emph{moment lift} is $L_2:\ x_i' = \mu^a + (\sigma^a/\sigma)(x_i-\mu)$. For a target response share $p^a$ under item map $\pi(x)=\mathrm{sigmoid}(\kappa x+c)$ and the operator's normalized within-segment weights $\omega_i$, the \emph{response lift} is $L_3:\ x_i' = x_i + \ell_i\,\eta^\ast$ where $\eta^\ast$ solves $\sum_i \omega_i\,\pi(x_i+\ell_i\eta)=p^a$.
\end{definition}
$L_1$ with $\ell_i=w_i/\bar w$ and with $\ell_i=1$ are ablations.

\begin{proposition}[Loading shift: minimum change, and its relation to the exact Kalman update]
\label{prop:lift}
(a) $L_1$ is the unique minimizer of $\sum_i (x_i'-x_i)^2/\ell_i$ subject to $\frac1n\sum_i x_i' = \mu^a$.
(b) Suppose the forecast distribution within a segment satisfies Equation~\eqref{eq:factor} with population (infinite-ensemble) covariances, and observations are linear or linearized, $y=\sum_{i\in s} g_i x_i + y_{-s} + c + \epsilon$ with $g_i\in\R^p$, where $y_{-s}$, the contribution of all other segments, is independent of this segment's beliefs, and $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R)$. Let $\tilde R=R+\Var(y_{-s})$, $G=\sum_i \ell_i g_i$, $S_y=\sigma_\eta^2GG^\top+\sigma_\zeta^2\sum_i g_ig_i^\top+\tilde R$ and $d=y-\E y$. Then the exact Kalman increment of agent $i$ is
\begin{equation}
\Delta x_i = \ell_i\,\Delta\eta + u_i,\qquad \Delta\eta=\sigma_\eta^2G^\top S_y^{-1}d,\qquad u_i=\sigma_\zeta^2\,g_i^\top S_y^{-1}d .
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Stationarity of the Lagrangian gives $(x_i'-x_i)/\ell_i=\lambda$, so $x_i'=x_i+\lambda\ell_i$, and the constraint with $\bar\ell=1$ gives $\lambda=\delta$. The objective is strictly convex, so the minimizer is unique. (b) $\Cov(x_i,x_j)=\ell_i\ell_j\sigma_\eta^2+\mathbb{1}\{i=j\}\sigma_\zeta^2$, so $\Cov(x_i,y)=\ell_i\sigma_\eta^2G^\top+\sigma_\zeta^2g_i^\top$ and $\Var(y)=S_y$. The Kalman increment is $\Cov(x_i,y)S_y^{-1}d$, and $\Cov(\eta,y)S_y^{-1}d=\Delta\eta$.
\end{proof}

Part (a) is a design choice: the metric is chosen so that its minimizer has the form of the first term in (b). Part (b) is standard Kalman algebra on a factor model, in the spirit of reduced-state-space Kalman filters~\cite{cane1996}; we use it to characterize when $L_1$ is close to exact, not as a new result. $L_1$ applies $\ell_i\delta$, and $\delta$, the analysed shift of the segment mean, already contains the mean idiosyncratic term: $\delta=\Delta\eta+\bar u$ (using $\bar\ell=1$). The gap between $L_1$ and the exact increment is therefore $\ell_i\bar u-u_i$, not $u_i$. For an observation whose weights on this segment's agents are $\omega_i$, write $\tilde\omega_i$ for the weights rescaled to mean one and $\bar\ell_\omega$ for the $\omega$-weighted mean loading. Then $g_i\propto\omega_i$, and the gap relative to the shift $\ell_i\Delta\eta$ is
\[
\frac{\sigma_\zeta^2}{n_s\sigma_\eta^2}\cdot\frac{\ell_i-\tilde\omega_i}{\ell_i\,\bar\ell_\omega}.
\]
It vanishes when loadings equal the normalized operator weights and is otherwise of relative order $\sigma_\zeta^2/(n_s\sigma_\eta^2)$, scaled by the mismatch between loading and weight. Combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:noise}, A1 is justified in the window
\[
\ell_i^2\,\mathrm{corr}(\eta,y)^2\,(M-1)\ <\ \sigma_\zeta^2/\sigma_\eta^2\ \ll\ n_s/|\ell_i-\tilde\omega_i| ,
\]
where per-agent sampling noise dominates a per-agent filter and $L_1$'s omitted term is small. For a single-segment poll ($|\mathrm{corr}|\approx1$) at $n_s\approx200$ with loading--weight mismatch of order one, the window runs from about $49\ell_i^2$ to well below 200, which is narrow. At the 50-agent merge floor of Appendix~\ref{app:inputs} it is nearly empty, unless loadings track operator weights or the observation aggregates many segments (which lowers the left bound). E1 reports the measured $\sigma_\zeta/\sigma_\eta$ by segment-size stratum against both bounds, alongside H3 and H7. An exploratory ablation, A1g, adds the omitted term $u_i-\ell_i\bar u$ computed from a pooled estimate of $\sigma_\zeta^2$; this needs no per-agent sample covariance and removes the right bound. The proposition does not cover finite ensembles, multi-factor perturbations or correlated $\zeta$ from the social graph. Nonlinear operators enter only through the linearization weights $g_i$, which depend on within-segment spread; that is how the spread dependence of \S\ref{sec:obs} appears. H7 tests the loading structure directly against a 500-member ensemble.

\begin{proposition}[Spurious heterogeneity from per-agent gains]
\label{prop:noise}
Under Equation~\eqref{eq:factor}, let $p$ observations be assimilated jointly with predicted values $Y^{(m)}\in\R^p$, sample covariance $C_Y$ and error covariance $R$, and suppose the $Y^{(m)}$ are independent of the $\zeta_i^{(m)}$ (neglecting each agent's own $O(g_i)$ contribution). Let the per-agent ensemble gain be $\hat K_i=\widehat{\Cov}(x_i,Y)(C_Y+R)^{-1}$. Then $\hat K_i=\ell_i\widehat{\Cov}(\eta,Y)(C_Y+R)^{-1}+e_i$, where, conditional on the $Y^{(m)}$, the $e_i$ are independent across agents with mean zero. If innovations are consistent, $\E\,dd^\top=C_Y+R$, the mean increments $\hat K_i d$ add to within-segment variance an independent component with expected variance
\[
\frac{\sigma_\zeta^2}{M-1}\,\tr\!\big((C_Y+R)^{-1}C_Y\big),
\]
which for one observation is $k\sigma_\zeta^2/(M-1)$ with $k=s_Y^2/(s_Y^2+r)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
See Appendix~\ref{app:proof}.
\end{proof}

The trace equals $\sum_o k_o$ only when the predicted observations are uncorrelated across the ensemble; correlated observations share the same $\zeta$ samples and add less (for $p$ identical observations it is $pc/(pc+r)$, not $pc/(c+r)$). The added component is uncorrelated with true individual beliefs, so it lowers rank correlation with the truth. Over days it accumulates. Write $v_{\mathrm{day}}$ for the daily value above and $a=1-\rho$ for the per-day decay under Equation~\eqref{eq:fj}, ignoring peer coupling (which lowers it). If successive days' spurious increments were independent, the added variance would settle at $v_{\mathrm{day}}/(1-a^2)=v_{\mathrm{day}}/(\rho(2-\rho))$. They are not: the anchor noise $\nu_i^{(m)}$ in $\zeta$ persists, and innovations are serially correlated when the model is biased. With lag correlation $\varrho^{|j|}$ between increments the steady state is $v_{\mathrm{day}}(1+a\varrho)/\big((1-a^2)(1-a\varrho)\big)$, rising to $v_{\mathrm{day}}/\rho^2$ for fully persistent increments. These are approximations, not exact results. H3 compares B4's observed excess with this interval, evaluated at the measured $\varrho$. $L_0$ has the same mean increment and also contracts each agent's idiosyncratic member spread by mixing members, so its effect on within-segment variance can have either sign; its effect on rank correlation does not. $L_1$ uses the segment-mean gain, in which $\zeta$ averages to variance $\sigma_\zeta^2/n_s$; the noise this leaves is a common shift, scaled by $\ell_i$, not independent per-agent noise. This is the mechanism H2 and H3 test.

\begin{proposition}[What each lift preserves]
\label{prop:rank}
(a) Under $L_1$, $x_i'-x_j'=x_i-x_j+\delta(\ell_i-\ell_j)$, so within-segment ranks are preserved whenever $\delta(\ell_i-\ell_j)$ does not reverse a gap, and within-segment variance changes by $\delta^2\Var(\ell)+2\delta\Cov(x,\ell)$; with equal loadings both are preserved exactly. (b) Among all reassignments $x'$ of the segment (any coupling of old and new values) whose output has mean $\mu^a$ and standard deviation $\sigma^a$, $L_2$ uniquely minimizes $\frac1n\sum_i(x_i'-x_i)^2$. It preserves every within-segment rank and, applied topic by topic, every within-segment correlation between topics. (c) For $L_3$, the left side of its defining equation is continuous and strictly increasing in $\eta$ with limits $0$ and $1$, so $\eta^\ast$ exists, is unique for any $p^a\in(0,1)$, and can be found by bisection; agents with equal $\ell_i$ keep their order.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
(a) Direct expansion. (b) $\frac1n\sum_i(x_i'-x_i)^2=(\mu^a-\mu)^2+\sigma^{a2}+\sigma^2-2\,\widehat{\Cov}(x,x')$, and by Cauchy--Schwarz $\widehat{\Cov}(x,x')\le\sigma\sigma^a$ with equality if and only if $x'$ is an increasing affine function of $x$, which with the two moment constraints is $L_2$. Positive affine maps preserve order, and correlation is invariant under a positive affine map of each coordinate. (c) Each summand is a composition of the increasing sigmoid with $x_i+\ell_i\eta$, $\ell_i>0$, $\omega_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\omega_i=1$; the limits follow termwise.
\end{proof}

The variance change in (a) is a deterministic function of the loadings, not independent noise. Because $\ell_i$ depends on $\bar x_i$ through the covariate $a_i+b-\bar x_i$, $\Cov(x,\ell)$ is generally nonzero, so $L_1$ itself adds or removes some within-segment spread; E1 reports this term per segment. In one dimension any increasing map is the optimal transport map onto its own image~\cite{peyre2019}, so an optimal-transport description of $L_2$ adds nothing beyond (b). $L_2$ ignores the loadings and so conflicts with the metric behind $L_1$.

\begin{remark}
No lift makes individual beliefs more accurate. They preserve the within-segment structure that population synthesis produced, which is only as good as that synthesis and the variance correction applied to it~\cite{rw_variance}. A lift that neither adds spurious spread nor erases real spread is necessary, because LLM-seeded populations already misstate spread~\cite{bisbee2024,ozkan2026,wang2024flatten}, but it is not sufficient. In particular, $L_1$ cannot correct a wrong within-segment spread: when spread is collapsed and shares are matched, the only free quantity is the mean, so A1 will move segment means to wrong values to fit the shares (\S\ref{sec:limits}; E1 spread-mismatch arm).
\end{remark}

$L_1$ is the default. $L_2$ is used only when $\sigma^a$ is constrained by data (ordinal response distributions); otherwise rescaling by an analysed spread would let ensemble noise act on heterogeneity. $L_3$ is used when the observation is a share and the segment is skewed or bimodal enough that matching the mean does not match the share; the bimodality rule that switches it on in A2 is in Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}.

\subsection{Discrete regimes as a mixture of sub-ensembles}
\label{sec:pf}
Some uncertainty is discrete: whether an ambiguous event is read as an attack or an accident, or whether a scandal has broken trust in an institution. We keep $J\le3$ regimes, each with its own sub-ensemble of $M_r$ equally weighted members whose forcing or parameters differ, and a probability $\pi_r$ per regime. Regime switches follow a Markov prior with a small switch probability, raised on days the news reader flags as ambiguous. Each day, \emph{before} any update, $\pi_r$ is multiplied by the forecast predictive likelihood of a small set of regime-sensitive observations, $\mathcal{N}\big(y^{\mathrm{reg}}_t;\ \bar Y_r,\ Y_rY_r^\top/(M_r-1)+R^{\mathrm{reg}}\big)$, computed from regime $r$'s forecast sub-ensemble. Each sub-ensemble is then updated by its own ETKF with all observations, including the regime-sensitive ones. This is the interacting multiple model construction, one filter per Markov regime mixed by predictive likelihoods~\cite{blom1988}, with an ensemble filter in each regime as in Gaussian-mixture ensemble filters~\cite{stordal2011}; it is the factorization $p(r\mid y)\,p(x\mid r,y)$ of a Rao--Blackwellised filter~\cite{doucet2000}, so the regime-sensitive observations are used once for the regime weight and once for the conditional state, as the factorization requires, not counted twice. Unlike the full interacting multiple model filter, we do not mix sub-ensemble states across regimes at each step; a switch is represented by re-seeding. Sub-ensembles have fixed size and equal internal weights, so the ETKF never sees unequal member weights. A regime whose probability falls below $0.01$ is retired, and its members are re-seeded as copies of the most probable regime's members with fresh draws of anchor noise and parameter perturbations from \S\ref{sec:init}. Forecast probabilities are mixtures $\sum_r\pi_rP_r$. Restricting the likelihood to a few observations limits weight collapse~\cite{snyder2008}; regimes are used only where a continuous parameter would not do. This is the ``particles are worlds, not agents'' pattern that scaled in crowd simulation~\cite{makinoshima2022}.

\section{Observation operators}
\label{sec:obs}

Each operator below is evaluated on every member's full population (Algorithm~1, step 2). Table~\ref{tab:obs} summarizes them.

\paragraph{Polls.} A poll $o$ asks a binary item mapped to topic $k(o)$ of a target population with post-stratification weights $\omega^o_i$ over agents, fielded on days $t_1,\dots,t_2$ and released on day $t_3\ge t_2$. Agent $i$'s response probability on day $\tau$ is $\pi_{o,i,\tau} = \mathrm{sigmoid}(\kappa_o x_{i,k(o),\tau} + c_o + \beta_{\mathrm{house}(o)})$, with $(\kappa_o,c_o)$ fixed as in \S\ref{sec:params} and $\beta_{\mathrm{house}}$ an augmented house effect, as in state-space poll pooling~\cite{jackman2005}. The poll is assimilated on its release day with operator and error
\begin{equation}
h_o = \logit\Big(\frac{1}{t_2-t_1+1}\sum_{\tau=t_1}^{t_2}\sum_i \omega^o_i\,\pi_{o,i,\tau}\Big),\qquad
r_o = \frac{\mathrm{deff}_o}{n_o\,\bar p_o(1-\bar p_o)} + \tau^2_{\mathrm{item}},
\end{equation}
by the delta method on the logit of a share with design effect $\mathrm{deff}_o$ and sample size $n_o$, plus a nonsampling floor $\tau^2_{\mathrm{item}}$~\cite{shiranimehr2018}. Here $\bar p_o$ is the forecast ensemble mean of the share, not the observed share, so that the error variance does not depend on the observed value. The operator is evaluated on each member's stored daily populations, so the analysis on day $t_3$ uses the ensemble covariance between fieldwork-time predicted observations and the present state. This is asynchronous (four-dimensional) ensemble assimilation~\cite{hunt2004}; it assumes the ensemble's perturbation structure evolves approximately linearly over the lag between fieldwork and release, typically a few days. Members keep 14 daily snapshots (about 5.6 GB in single precision at $M=50$); fieldwork days older than that are dropped from the average. Because $\pi$ is nonlinear, the share depends on within-segment spread as well as the mean: a population with collapsed variance and the right mean log-odds produces the wrong share. Evaluating $h_o$ on agents keeps this dependence explicit, and it is why variance collapse is an observation-operator error, not only a realism problem. Ordinal items use cumulative-logit cutpoints in place of $c_o$ and observe the full response distribution, which is what constrains $\sigma_{s,k}$ in A2.

\paragraph{Finite-population error.} A finite synthetic population's weighted share differs from the share of the infinite population it stands for, with variance about $r^{\mathrm{rep}}_o=\sum_i(\omega^o_i)^2\Var_{\omega}(\pi_{o,\cdot})/\big(\bar p_o^2(1-\bar p_o)^2\big)$ on the logit scale, where $\Var_\omega$ is the weighted variance of $\pi_{o,i}$ over the target population. Every member and every day share the same agents, so this error is persistent, not white noise, and it is not in $r_o$. Its segment-level part is absorbed by the offsets $b_s$; the rest is reported per country as a diagnostic, largest for countries at the 100-agent floor, and adding it to $r_o$ is a sensitivity variant. Methods without agents (B1, B3, B3m) have no such term.

\paragraph{Prediction markets.} A market price is not a belief measurement; it is traders' probability for an outcome. We assimilate a market only if its outcome is determined by population beliefs at resolution time $T_e$, such as a referendum: the outcome is $\mathbb{1}\{\Psi_e(X_{T_e})>c_e\}$ with
\[
\Psi_e(X)=\frac{\sum_i \omega^e_i\,\tau_i\,\pi_{e,i}}{\sum_i\omega^e_i\,\tau_i},
\]
where $\omega^e$ restricts to eligible voters and $\tau_i$ is a turnout propensity from covariates, fixed from published turnout aggregates by country (Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}); a validated turnout model is outside this paper's scope. The operator is defined per member, from that member's current state. Let $\Psi^{(m)}_{e,t}=\Psi_e(X^{(m)}_t)$ and $\Psi^{\infty(m)}_e$ its value at the member's anchors $a^{(m)}+b^{(m)}$. We project to $T_e$ under the linearized mean reversion of Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} without future news, with $\Delta=T_e-t$ and a fixed pull $\bar\rho$ (the prior mean of $\rho$), and represent future news by a forcing variance:
\begin{equation}
\hat\Psi^{(m)}_e=\Psi^{\infty(m)}_e+\big(\Psi^{(m)}_{e,t}-\Psi^{\infty(m)}_e\big)(1-\bar\rho)^{\Delta},\qquad
s_e^2=q_e\,\frac{1-(1-\bar\rho)^{2\Delta}}{1-(1-\bar\rho)^2},
\end{equation}
where $q_e$ is the per-day forcing variance of $\Psi_e$, estimated from the day-to-day variability of comparable poll series before the evaluation window and not from ensemble spread, so that ensemble underdispersion does not feed back into market probabilities. Then
\begin{equation}
h_e^{(m)} = \beta_{0,e} + \logit\Phi\big((\hat\Psi^{(m)}_e-c_e)/s_e\big),
\end{equation}
with $\Phi$ the standard normal CDF, the slope fixed at one and the intercept $\beta_{0,e}$ given a shared zero-mean prior (\S\ref{sec:params}). Its ensemble spread comes from member differences in current beliefs and offsets, so the price updates $\mu$ and $b$ through an ordinary synchronous ensemble covariance; members are not run ahead for the operator. Because $\bar\rho$ and $q_e$ are fixed, the price carries no information about $\rho$, and $s_e$ does not represent uncertainty in the reversion rate. The error variance is $r_e=r_e^{\mathrm{spread}}+\tau^2_{\mathrm{mkt}}$: the squared half bid--ask spread on the logit scale, plus a structural term for what the price reflects and the model omits (candidate events, turnout shocks, traders' own information). Without $\tau^2_{\mathrm{mkt}}$, a liquid market with a narrow spread would dominate the analysis. $\tau_{\mathrm{mkt}}$ is tuned under the common budget for arm M1. Markets on outcomes not determined by population beliefs (a central bank decision, a court ruling) are never assimilated and never forecast by this method.

\paragraph{Question probabilities.} Algorithm~1 step 8 produces probabilities only for belief-determined questions: (i) poll-threshold questions (``will the next poll of item $o$ in country $c$ show more than $p^\ast$''), using the poll operator's predictive mixture over members including $r_o$, with the forcing variance above added for leads past seven days; and (ii) vote outcomes, using $\frac1M\sum_m\Phi\big((\hat\Psi^{(m)}_e-c_e)/s_e\big)$. Both are smooth mixtures, not member fractions, which with $M=50$ would move in steps of $0.02$ and reach $0$ and $1$. Probabilities are clipped to $[0.01,0.99]$ before scoring, and no post-hoc recalibration enters any primary score. Other question types in the benchmark~\cite{rw_bench} are outside this method.

\paragraph{News as forcing, not observation.} GDELT records what outlets published, coded by machine~\cite{leetaru2013}; its volume tracks media output, not public belief. We never treat news as an observation of $X$. Each story is read once by an LLM into a signed signal on the topics it touches, with country and language tags, giving $\hat F_t$. Reading uncertainty is estimated by reading each story several times with different prompts and models and taking the spread $\Sigma_F$, sampled per member in step 1. The magnitude with which news moves beliefs is the gain $\lambda$, which the filter estimates; the LLM chooses direction and topic, not size. This separation matters because networks of LLM agents left to update one another converge toward the model's view of the truth~\cite{chuang2023}. Stories whose main content is a poll result, a polling average or prediction-market odds are removed from the forcing in every arm by a fixed classifier and keyword list (Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}); a story the classifier cannot score is removed. Polls already enter as observations, and keeping their reports in the forcing would count them twice. For the market-blind arm, removing reports of market odds is what makes the arm market-blind (\S\ref{sec:e3}).

\paragraph{Search interest.} Google Trends divides each data point by total searches in its geography and time range, rescales to 0--100, and computes values from a sample of searches~\cite{googletrendsfaq}, so only relative changes within a series are informative, and the sampling noise can be substantial~\cite{eichenauer2022}. The latent salience $\chi_{c,k}$ per country and topic multiplies exposure in Equation~\eqref{eq:fj}, is part of the reduced state as a random walk in $\log\chi$, and is observed as $\log(1+y_{c,k,t}) = \beta_{c,k,t} + \log \chi_{c,k,t} + \epsilon$, with a per-series offset $\beta_{c,k,t}$ that follows a slower random walk (identified as in \S\ref{sec:params}, item 5). Each value is the mean of several pulls, and the variance between pulls is added to the error variance. Search-based nowcasts have worked~\cite{choi2012,ginsberg2009} and have failed when the search-to-target relationship drifted~\cite{lazer2014}; the drifting offset is the guard, and the operator is kept only if the E2 ablation shows it helps.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

No implementation of A1 exists, so this section contains no results for it; the committed results on the engine's simpler operator are in \S\ref{sec:prior}. It specifies the hypotheses, baselines, metrics, data, analysis plan and decision criteria. This is a specification, not yet a registration: the protocol file adds the values that the rules of Appendix~\ref{app:deferred} produce (series, countries, items, dates, prior scales), and it will be hashed, its hash published with a public timestamp following~\cite{rw_receipts}, and the commit referenced in this paper before the first E1 run. Every quantity still free after this paper is listed in Appendix~\ref{app:deferred} with the mechanical rule that fixes it. Any deviation from the protocol will be reported as a deviation.

\subsection{Methods compared}
\begin{description}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item[B0 Free run.] The same ensemble and forecast model with no assimilation.
\item[B1 Persistence.] The last observed value of each observed quantity, carried forward.
\item[B2 Nudging.] For each observation, shift the segment means in its target population along the operator's gradient, $\Delta\mu_s=\kappa_{\mathrm{n}}\,(y_o-h_o(\bar X))\,g_s/\|g\|^2$ with $g_s=\partial h_o/\partial\mu_s$ evaluated on the ensemble-mean population, which is the smallest shift that moves $h_o$ by the fraction $\kappa_{\mathrm{n}}$ of the innovation to first order~\cite{hoke1976}; the gain $\kappa_{\mathrm{n}}$ is tuned; then lift with $L_1$.
\item[B3 Poll aggregator.] A Gaussian random-walk state-space model per country and item with house effects~\cite{jackman2005}, identified as in \S\ref{sec:params}; no agents and no pooling across countries.
\item[B3m Dynamic MRP.] A multilevel logistic regression of poll responses on segment covariates with country, item and random-walk time effects pooled across countries and items, post-stratified to each poll's target population~\cite{park2004mrp,ghitza2013,wang2015xbox}; no agents.
\item[B4 Per-agent ETKF.] The ensemble-weight lift $L_0$: the per-agent ETKF with the same blocks, localization, inflation and $N=10^5$ as A1.
\item[B4s Shrunk per-agent ETKF.] B4 with each agent's gain shrunk toward the structured gain, $\hat K_i^{\mathrm{shr}}=(1-\varsigma)\hat K_i+\varsigma\,\hat\ell_i\hat K_\mu$, with $\varsigma\in[0,1]$ tuned; $\varsigma=1$ reduces to the mean increment of A1.
\item[B5 World particle filter.] A bootstrap particle filter whose particles are whole worlds~\cite{gordon1993,makinoshima2022}, at matched member-days of forecast-model runs. Exploratory.
\item[B6 Segment-only model.] The same LETKF on a segment-level version of Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} with no individual agents. Each segment and topic carries a mean and a variance. The mean follows the segment average of Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} with $\gamma=0$, which is exact when there is no coupling. The variance is $\sigma^2_{s,k,t}=\sigma^2_{a,s,k}+D_{s,k,t}$, with $D_{t+1}=(1-\rho)^2D_t+\lambda^2F_t^2\Var_s(w \chi E)+\sigma_\xi^2$; this anchor spread plus accumulated deviation ignores covariances between anchors and news response. Poll operators use the moment closure $p\approx\mathrm{sigmoid}\big((\kappa\mu+c)/\sqrt{1+\pi\kappa^2\sigma^2/8}\big)$~\cite{mackay1992}. B6 therefore differs from A1 only through the shape of within-segment distributions, through exact versus closed-form shares, and through coupling when $\gamma\ne0$.
\item[B7 public filter.] The public build's filter when the protocol file is frozen: the iterated extended Kalman filter of~\cite{rw_askaworld} (per segment, on polls, diagonal covariance across segments, no social graph and no market observations) if it has replaced the engine's scalar Kalman update by then, otherwise that scalar update (\S\ref{sec:prior}), with its process-noise and observation-error scalings tuned under the common budget.
\item[A1] Ours: reduced LETKF, $L_1$ with estimated loadings, parameter augmentation.
\item[A1w, A1u, A1g] A1 with $\ell_i=w_i/\bar w$, with $\ell_i=1$, and with the idiosyncratic correction of \S\ref{sec:lift}. Exploratory.
\item[A1c] A1 with a structured gain one level up: within each country block, segment means are updated through a country factor with estimated segment loadings, by the same construction as $L_1$. Exploratory.
\item[A2] A1 with $\log\sigma$ in the state, $L_2$ where ordinal data exist and $L_3$ for skewed segments. Exploratory.
\item[A3] A1 plus the regime mixture. Used only for the regime part of H6.
\end{description}

\subsection{Statistical conventions}
\label{sec:conventions}
\paragraph{Primary hypotheses and multiplicity.} There are five primary hypotheses: H1 and H2 (E1), H8 and H10 (E2), and H12 (E3). The family-wise error rate over every hypothesis in the paper, primary and secondary, is controlled at $0.05$ in the strong sense by a graphical procedure~\cite{bretz2009}. The graph is as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item \emph{Initial weights:} $1/5$ on each primary, so each is tested at $\alpha=0.01$; zero on every secondary. Bonferroni over the primaries remains valid although they are analysed at different times.
\item \emph{Transitions:} a rejected E1 primary passes its level in equal parts to the E1 secondary family $\{$H3, H4, H5, H6a, H6b, H7, H14$\}$; a rejected E2 primary passes its level in equal parts to $\{$H9, H11a, H11b, H11c$\}$; a rejected H12 passes its level to H13.
\item \emph{Within a family:} a rejected secondary passes its level in equal parts to the family's remaining members, which is Holm's step-down procedure~\cite{holm1979} at the propagated level. There are no transitions across experiments or back to primaries.
\end{itemize}
An intersection--union hypothesis (H1, H5, H6a, H8, H10) is rejected only if every component rejects at that hypothesis's level, which needs no further adjustment~\cite{bergerhsu1996}. Exploratory analyses are reported with estimates and intervals and no test. Exact sign-flip tests over $n$ units cannot give $p<2^{-n}$. The smallest level a secondary can receive is $0.01/7\approx0.0014$ in E1 and $0.01/4=0.0025$ in E2, so every tested E1 arm has at least 10 test seeds ($2^{-10}\approx0.00098$), and E2 requires at least 10 countries.

\paragraph{Tuning budget and freezing.} Every method with hyperparameters gets the same budget: 30 configurations from a random search over its own knobs, scored on the tuning split only. For every ensemble method the knobs include $\sigma_\xi$ (chosen by innovation consistency, \S\ref{sec:setup}); for A1 they also include $\alpha$, $\phi$, the localization radius and the random-walk scales $c$; for B2 the gain; for B4s $\varsigma$ plus B4's knobs; for B3, B3m and B7 their prior and noise scales; for arm M1 also $\tau_{\mathrm{mkt}}$. $\sigma_{b,\tau}$, $\sigma_\nu$ and $\sigma_w$ are fixed externally (\S\ref{sec:init}) and tuned by no method. E1 uses seeds 1--5 for tuning and seeds 6--25 for testing. E2 uses the first 60 days of its window for tuning and the rest for testing. After E2, the E3 configuration is frozen, hashed and published before the first E3 forecast. It is A1 minus any component whose H11 test does not reject; if H11 is not tested because neither E2 primary rejects, no component is removed. The E3 configuration is fixed whatever E2's outcome, so E3 runs even if H8 fails. Because H11 selects components on the E2 test split, E2's headline numbers are for the pre-ablation configuration.

\paragraph{Predictive distributions.} Scores against observations (E2, E3) use the observable each method predicts, under a predictive distribution that includes observation error. Ensemble methods (B0, B2, B4, B4s, B5, B6, A1--A3): the mixture $\frac1M\sum_m\mathcal{N}(h_o(X^{(m)}),r_o)$. B1: $\mathcal{N}(\text{last observed logit share},\ r_{\mathrm{last}}+r_o+q\,\Delta t)$, with $q$ the per-day variance of the series estimated on the tuning split. B3, B3m and B7: their posterior predictive plus $r_o$. $r_o$ contains no finite-population term (\S\ref{sec:obs}), so it is the same for methods with and without agents. Log scores and CRPS are proper scoring rules for these predictive distributions~\cite{gneiting2007}. E1 scores against the true state, which carries no observation error, so E1's segment-mean forecasts are scored without $r_o$. For them we report both the fair ensemble CRPS~\cite{ferro2014}, which assumes exchangeable independent members, and the standard ensemble CRPS, because deterministic square-root members are not independent.

\subsection{Committed results on the engine's operator}
\label{sec:prior}
These results are not E1: they test the operator the engine runs today, not A1, on a smaller and simpler synthetic world, and they were registered in the Real World repository's experiment registry (\nolinkurl{experiments/REGISTRY.md}), not under this paper's protocol file. Both use a 2,000-agent engine world with its country $\times$ age band $\times$ education segments, 5 topics, a truth that walks in logit with standard deviation 0.05 per day and shocks on days 10 and 20, 20\% of segment--topic cells polled daily with logit noise 0.2, no news forcing, and a prior biased from the truth; 50 seeds of 30 days; 95\% bootstrap intervals over seeds. Differences are day-30 RMSE of segment means in log-odds, paired by seed; negative favours the first arm. Every pre-registered metric is listed.

\emph{E003, capped nudging}, on seeds 0--49 (run at commit \texttt{b6957be}; artifact \nolinkurl{experiments/E003/results/2026-10-12-none/results.json}). Nudging minus no assimilation $-0.4449$ ($-0.4497$ to $-0.4401$, $n=50$), its pre-registered success; nudging minus persistence $+0.2353$ ($+0.2288$ to $+0.2419$, $n=50$), a loss; never-observed cells, nudging minus none, $+0.0000$ ($n=32$); zero-evidence variance check passed. The ETKF arm was dropped because the engine has no ETKF, and the spread--skill ratio was not computed because nudging runs one member. Deviations: 137 segments on seed~0 instead of the planned 200; no synthetic market aggregate.

\emph{E006, the scalar Kalman update with anchor offset}, on seeds 1000--1049 (run at commit \texttt{3163def}; artifact \nolinkurl{experiments/E006/results/2026-10-12-none/RESULTS.md}). Minus persistence $-0.0182$ ($-0.0190$ to $-0.0174$, $n=50$), its pre-registered success; minus nudging $-0.2608$ ($-0.2675$ to $-0.2543$); minus no assimilation $-0.7118$ ($-0.7197$ to $-0.7038$); never-observed cells $+0.0000$ ($n=33$); walk standard deviation halved and doubled, minus persistence, $-0.0174$ ($-0.0182$ to $-0.0165$) and $-0.0190$ ($-0.0197$ to $-0.0183$); day-30 RMSE over the filter's own predicted standard deviation, mean 1.367 (range 1.198 to 1.612), so the filter is overconfident; largest change in within-segment standard deviation $9.13\times10^{-13}$; zero-evidence check passed; no deviations. On that result, as the repository's decision record \nolinkurl{research/decisions/0005-assimilation-v2.md} required, the engine's default switched from nudging to this update.

What these show is narrow. Each poll observes one segment mean directly, so there is no aggregation, no Jensen gap and no question of how to lift a country-level increment to segments, which is what this paper is about. The truth's walk matches the filter's assumed walk, poll noise is known, and the update was developed on E003's seeds 0--19. Its margin over persistence is small, and its overconfidence is the failure that inflation and spread--skill monitoring address in A1. In the daily cycle the same update acts on country means, not segments.

\subsection{E1: synthetic truth}
\label{sec:e1}
\paragraph{Design.} \emph{Identical twin (main arm).} The truth runs Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} on a stylized $10^5$-agent population with $S=500$ segments ($n_s\approx200$), $K=20$ topics, $\theta^\ast$ drawn from the prior, a 30-day spin-up and 120 scored days. Forcing is synthetic: each day a Poisson number of stories (mean 20) each touch one topic in one to three countries with a standard normal signal; the filter receives the signal plus reading error with standard deviation half the signal's, and its $\Sigma_F$ equals the true reading variance (half of it in a sensitivity arm). The truth population shares the filter's covariates, so agents correspond and individual comparisons are defined, but its anchors and susceptibilities are perturbed: segment-level anchor error with standard deviation $0.3$ log-odds, individual anchor error with standard deviation half the within-segment standard deviation of $a$, and $\log w$ error with standard deviation $0.3$. The filter starts from its own population and never sees the perturbation. Its ensemble scales $\sigma_{b,\tau}$, $\sigma_\nu$, $\sigma_w$ are set by the external rule of Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}, not to these values, so E1 does not hand the filter its true error statistics. A \emph{matched-statistics} arm, with the filter's scales set to the truth's perturbation levels, is reported as a reference, and a perfect-population control without perturbation is reported for reference only. Synthetic observations follow real release calendars (dense in a few countries, monthly or absent in most), with $n_o\in[500,2000]$, fieldwork periods of 2--5 days and release 1--7 days after fieldwork ends. On days 15, 45, 75 and 105 a shock story arrives whose true effect is set by a regime: a gain multiplier of $-1$, $0$ or $+1$ on its signal for 30 days, drawn uniformly. A3's regime set is the same three, which is an oracle; an out-of-set arm in which the truth uses a multiplier of $2$ is exploratory.
\emph{Allocation arm.} As the main arm, but with countries, agent counts and segments from the allocation and merge rule of Appendix~\ref{app:inputs}, so most countries hold a few hundred agents and segments go down to 50; results are reported by segment-size stratum and for countries at the 100-agent floor.
\emph{Spread-mismatch arm.} The truth's within-segment anchor spread is scaled by $0.7$ and by $1.4$ relative to the filter's; we report the bias of A1's and A2's segment means.
\emph{Operator misspecification.} Synthetic polls carry house effects of standard deviation $0.1$ on the logit scale, item slopes multiplied by a factor uniform on $[0.8,1.25]$, and true error variances $1.5$ times those the filter assumes.
\emph{Fraternal twin.} The truth is the per-agent LLM display world of \S\ref{sec:setup}, run without write-back, on $2{,}000$ agents in ten countries, with $S=40$ ($n_s=50$), $K=5$, a 30-day spin-up, 60 scored days and 10 seeds. It reads real GDELT stories from a fixed past window. The truth's agents and story reader use a different model family from the filter's reader, so their reading errors are not shared. The filter runs Equation~\eqref{eq:fj}, so the forecast model is misspecified in a realistic way. The cost is one LLM call per truth agent per day, $2{,}000\times90\times10=1.8\times10^6$ calls, plus repeated calls to measure response variability; the measured cost will be reported.
\emph{Large-ensemble diagnostic.} Ten seeds of the main arm are rerun with $M=500$ to estimate per-agent covariances with little sampling noise.
\emph{Anchor-noise arm.} A1 and B4 are also run with $\sigma_\nu=0$.
\emph{Write-back coherence} (exploratory). In a separate run of the display world with write-back, we measure how far an LLM agent's next-day read-out moves from a written-back value on days without relevant news.

\paragraph{Metrics.} All E1 metrics compare with the true state, which has no observation error. Segment-level RMSE of $\mu_{s,k}$ in log-odds at analysis and at leads 1--7; ``7-day CRPS'' is the fair CRPS of forecast segment means averaged over leads 1--7, with lead 7 alone also reported; spread--skill ratio and rank histograms~\cite{hamill2001}. Heterogeneity metrics are computed on the \emph{ensemble-mean} analysis population $\bar x^a_i$, so they measure differences between agents, not spread between members: the within-segment variance ratio $\mathrm{VR}=\Var_i(\bar x^a_i)/\Var_i(x^{\mathrm{true}}_i)$ and the within-segment Spearman correlation between $\bar x^a_i$ and $x^{\mathrm{true}}_i$, for every segment, topic and analysis day; each seed's statistic is the median over segment $\times$ topic $\times$ day. Between-segment metrics, within each country: the variance of segment means relative to the truth's, and the Spearman correlation of segment means with the truth's. Also: the measured $\sigma_\zeta/\sigma_\eta$ by segment-size stratum, against the bounds of \S\ref{sec:lift}; the variance change $L_1$ itself introduces (Proposition~\ref{prop:rank}(a)); the variance excess predicted by Proposition~\ref{prop:noise}; posterior error and 90\% interval coverage for $\lambda,\gamma,\rho$; the posterior correlation of $b$ and $\mu$ and the long-run path of $b$; posterior mass on the true regime after each shock.

\paragraph{Hypotheses and decision criteria.} Seed is the unit of analysis. Tests on differences use the exact one-sided sign-flip permutation test over test seeds (20 in the main arm, 10 in the fraternal twin and the large-ensemble diagnostic); intervals use a seed-level bootstrap with $10^4$ resamples.
\begin{description}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item[H1 (primary, skill).] A1's 7-day CRPS is lower than both B0's and B2's (intersection--union; each at $\alpha=0.01$).
\item[H2 (primary, within-segment heterogeneity).] (a) The $1-2\alpha$ seed-bootstrap interval of the mean over seeds of the seed-level median of $\mathrm{VR}_{\mathrm{A1}}/\mathrm{VR}_{\mathrm{B0}}$ (both on the same seed, segment, topic and day) lies inside $[0.8,1.25]$; and (b) A1's seed-level median Spearman correlation is non-inferior to B0's with margin $0.02$ (one-sided lower bound above $-0.02$). Both are required. Absolute VRs are reported descriptively; the ratio removes the part of VR set by the E1 design (the truth's individual anchor error puts B0's VR near $0.8$). H2 is a no-harm test and is interpreted only together with H1, because a filter that barely moves agents passes it. The band is two-sided because a filter can erase spread (shrinking agents toward segment means) or add it (Proposition~\ref{prop:noise}); $[0.8,1.25]$ allows the within-segment standard deviation to change by at most about 12\%. Heterogeneity matters because LLM-seeded populations already vary too little~\cite{bisbee2024,ozkan2026}, so an update cannot afford to shrink it further. \emph{Positive control:} the same test is applied to A1 plus independent per-agent noise with the variance Proposition~\ref{prop:noise} predicts for B4 at the measured $\sigma_\zeta$, observations and $\rho$. If H2 also passes for this arm, H2(a) is reported as insensitive at that noise level and only H2(b) is interpreted.
\item[H3 (secondary, per-agent filter).] A1's seed-level median Spearman correlation exceeds B4's. If instead A1 and B4 are shown equivalent within $\pm0.02$ (two one-sided tests at the propagated level), the argument of \S\ref{sec:setup} is wrong for this regime and we will say so; if neither is shown, the result is inconclusive. B4s is reported alongside, with its tuned $\varsigma$, and both A1 and B4 are reported in the anchor-noise arm. B4's observed VR excess is reported against the interval predicted in \S\ref{sec:lift} from the measured $\sigma_\zeta$, $C_Y$, $\rho$ and lag correlation.
\item[H4 (secondary, no harm where unobserved).] For segment--topic pairs with no observation within the localization radius during the window, the one-sided upper confidence bound of A1's analysis RMSE divided by B0's is below $1.05$. Segments observed only through their country's aggregate are reported as a separate stratum.
\item[H5 (secondary, misspecification).] H1 and H2(b) hold in the fraternal twin (10 seeds).
\item[H6 (secondary, parameters and regimes).] (a) For each of $\lambda$, $\gamma$ and $\rho$, the seed-level squared error of A1's posterior mean after 60 days is below that of the prior mean (intersection--union over the three); 90\% interval coverage is reported. (b) A3 puts more than half its probability on the true regime within 7 days of a shock in more than half of shocks. The seed-level proportion over its four shocks, minus $0.5$, is tested by the sign-flip test.
\item[H7 (secondary, loading structure).] In the large-ensemble diagnostic, per-agent regression slopes $\hat b_i$ of $x_i$ on the segment mean are compared with $\hat\ell_i$ from the pooled loading model. The statistic is the noise-ceiling-corrected $R^2$: the $R^2$ of $\hat b_i$ on $\hat\ell_i$ divided by $1-\overline{\mathrm{se}^2_i}/\Var(\hat b_i)$, where $\mathrm{se}_i$ is each slope's standard error at $M=500$. The seed-level corrected $R^2$ minus $0.5$ is tested by the sign-flip test over 10 seeds. If the slope variance beyond sampling noise is less than 10\% of $\Var(\hat b_i)$, loadings are effectively homogeneous, H7 is reported as not applicable, and $\ell_i=1$ (A1u) is the recommended default.
\item[H14 (secondary, between-segment structure).] Within countries, A1's ratio of between-segment variance to the truth's, divided by B0's, has a $1-2\alpha$ interval inside $[0.8,1.25]$, and A1's within-country Spearman correlation of segment means with the truth is non-inferior to B0's with margin $0.02$. A1c is reported alongside.
\end{description}
\paragraph{Exploratory.} Sensitivity sweeps are one at a time around the tuned configuration, ten test seeds each: $M\in\{10,20,50,100\}$, inflation, localization radius, observation density, lift choice (A1w, A1u, A1g, A1c, A2), the susceptibility model ($\sigma_w$ doubled and halved), loading floor, global versus averaged $\theta$ updates, the reading-spread arm, the out-of-set regime arm and the operator-misspecification arm. The allocation, spread-mismatch and matched-statistics arms are reported in full. Spread--skill ratios inside $[0.8,1.2]$ at leads 1--7 are the stated target for A1. B5 and B7 are reported in E1 without tests.

\subsection{E2: real-data hindcast}
\label{sec:e2}
\paragraph{Data.} Recurring multi-country poll series (monthly online trackers and annual global attitude surveys), national vote-intention polls where published, GDELT event and knowledge-graph files, daily Kalshi and Polymarket price snapshots, and search interest, each used only where its terms permit (\S\ref{sec:ethics}). The inclusion rules for series, countries and items are in Appendix~\ref{app:deferred}.

\paragraph{Leakage and vintage control.} LLMs initialize anchors and read news, so the window must begin after the training cutoff of every model used; otherwise the filter could be credited with knowledge the model memorized~\cite{halawi2024,douven2026}. Anchors, item maps $(\kappa_o,c_o)$, turnout propensities and $q_e$ use only survey waves and data whose fieldwork ended before the window starts, and no wave used for anchors is among the held-out series. All tuning uses the tuning split. Each story is read with only that day's and earlier context.

\paragraph{Feasibility rule.} On the protocol freeze date we count, for the window, the held-out polls, the countries they cover and the window length after the latest model cutoff, and record the counts in the protocol file; we do not estimate them here. If the test split has fewer than 200 held-out polls or fewer than 10 countries, E2 is reported as underpowered and H8 and H10 are not claimed.

\paragraph{Primary metric.} Every method predicts the same target: a poll's fieldwork-averaged logit topline, from information available through the day before its release (including all polls released by then), under the predictive distributions of \S\ref{sec:conventions}. It is scored by the log score, with CRPS secondary; polls are never assimilated before they are scored. Also reported: the same with information through the day before fieldwork starts, at 7-day lead, and Brier and log scores on belief-determined market questions in the market-blind arm. Observation density is reported per country next to every number, and results are split by population tier.

\paragraph{Hypotheses.} The unit of analysis is the country. Tests use a country-level sign-flip randomization test: the statistic is the poll-weighted mean of country-level mean score differences, and its null distribution flips the sign of whole countries (all $2^C$ patterns for $C\le20$, otherwise $10^5$ random draws). With 10 countries the smallest attainable one-sided $p$ is $2^{-10}\approx0.00098$, below every level in the graph. Intervals are obtained by inverting the same test. Release-week blocks are a sensitivity analysis.
\begin{description}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item[H8 (primary).] A1's mean log score is higher than both B0's and B1's (intersection--union, each at $\alpha=0.01$).
\item[H9 (secondary).] A1 is non-inferior to the better of B3 and B3m in mean log score, margin $0.05$ nats per poll (one-sided lower bound above $-0.05$). For a Gaussian predictive distribution, $0.05$ nats is the expected loss from a mean bias of about $0.32$ predictive standard deviations, or from a predictive standard deviation about 27\% too wide. The subgroup of countries with fewer than one poll per month is exploratory unless it has at least 100 scored polls.
\item[H10 (primary, agents and the upgrade).] A1's mean log score is higher than both B6's (the same filter on a segment-only model) and B7's (the public filter), intersection--union, each at $\alpha=0.01$. With $\gamma=0$, A1 and B6 differ only through within-segment distribution shape (B6's description above), so the B6 comparison tests whether the distribution of beliefs within segments improves poll prediction. Decision rule: the product replaces v1 with A1 only if H10 is rejected. If A1 beats B7 but not B6, the evidence supports a segment-level ensemble filter, not agents, and we will say so. Non-rejection is not evidence of no difference: each comparison is reported with its interval, and A1 and B6 are declared equivalent only if that interval lies within $\pm0.05$ nats.
\item[H11 (secondary, ablations).] (a) Removing search observations, (b) removing anchor offsets and (c) setting the forcing reading spread to zero each lower the mean log score. A component is kept in the E3 configuration only if its test rejects; if H11 is not tested, every component is kept.
\end{description}

\subsection{E3: live prospective evaluation}
\label{sec:e3}
Each day the cycle runs and forecasts on belief-determined questions from the benchmark~\cite{rw_bench} are hash-committed before resolution~\cite{rw_receipts}. Forecasts are made and published daily, but each question is scored once, on the forecast committed for its scheduled call time; forecasts on other days are published and logged but enter no hypothesis. For each question we record the market price at the call time as~\cite{rw_receipts} defines it: the venue-history mid at the call time (Kalshi: closing bid and ask of the one-minute candle containing it; Polymarket: the one-minute bucket containing it). The venue is Kalshi if the question is listed there and Polymarket otherwise. Questions with no quote, a spread above 10 percentage points, or volume below the liquidity threshold of Appendix~\ref{app:deferred} are scored but excluded from the market comparison. We report Brier scores~\cite{brier1950} and log scores, and their differences from the market's, on every question, hits and misses alike. Belief-determined questions are referendums, elections and poll thresholds; on these venues they are few, cluster around a small number of elections and are concentrated in a few countries, mostly the United States. The market comparison will therefore cover a handful of countries, not the world, and H12 is likely to be demoted under the time limit below.

\paragraph{The market circularity.} If the filter assimilates a market and is scored against the same market, agreement with the market is rewarded trivially. We run two arms every day and publish both. \emph{Arm M0}, the market-blind arm, assimilates polls and search only, and its forcing excludes stories that report poll results or market odds (\S\ref{sec:obs}); if the upgrade is adopted as a new version, it is the configuration that would occupy the confirmatory market-blind slot (\texttt{rw-blind}) of~\cite{rw_receipts}; until then it is a separate, registered research slot, and H12 tests it on belief-determined questions only. \emph{Arm M1}, a market-aware arm, also assimilates belief-determined markets and can show value only through combination; it is compared with the market and with M0, and questions whose market enters M1's analysis are flagged. The story filter reduces market information in M0 but cannot remove it: events that move markets also move the news M0 reads, which is shared information rather than leakage, and some reports of odds will escape the filter. We therefore run a \emph{leakage audit}. Each month, 200 randomly drawn retained stories are hand-coded for poll or market-odds content, and the residual rate is published. The decision metric is the pooled residual rate of market-odds content: if its one-sided 95\% Clopper--Pearson upper bound exceeds 2\% when H12 is analysed, H12 is reported as compromised and not claimed. M0's daily probability changes are also regressed on the previous day's market price changes, controlling for poll releases; this regression cannot separate leakage from shared news, so its coefficient is reported descriptively.

\paragraph{Hypotheses, power and stopping.} Questions on the same event (for example, several thresholds on one election) are averaged into one event-level difference. Event-level differences are averaged by resolution week, and tests use the Diebold--Mariano statistic~\cite{dieboldmariano1995} on the weekly series with a Newey--West variance~\cite{neweywest1987} whose lag, in weeks, is the longest forecast horizon divided by seven, rounded up. A moving-block bootstrap over resolution weeks is a sensitivity analysis.
\begin{description}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item[H12 (primary).] Arm M0 is non-inferior to the market price with a relative margin: the one-sided $1-\alpha$ upper bound of the mean of $\mathrm{BS}_{\mathrm{M0},e}-1.05\,\mathrm{BS}_{\mathrm{mkt},e}$ is below zero at $\alpha=0.01$, equivalently a Brier skill score against the market above $-0.05$. In Prophet Arena the market baseline's Brier score was $0.187$ and five LLMs scored from $0.184$ to $0.219$~\cite{yang2025prophet}; at that market level the margin is about $0.009$, which would count the fourth of those five ($0.197$, $0.010$ worse than the market) as inferior and the third ($0.194$) as non-inferior. A fixed margin of $0.02$, as in our previous draft, would have counted all but the last as non-inferior.
\item[H13 (secondary).] Arm M0's mean Brier score is lower than a live free run B0's.
\end{description}
The analysis time is fixed by power, not by a round number. Before freezing E3 we estimate the standard deviation $s_d$ of event-level differences $\mathrm{BS}_{\mathrm{M0}}-1.05\,\mathrm{BS}_{\mathrm{mkt}}$ from E2's market-blind arm on belief-determined markets, and set $n^\ast=\big((z_{0.99}+z_{0.8})\,s_d/(0.05\,\overline{\mathrm{BS}}_{\mathrm{mkt}})\big)^2$, which gives 80\% power when M0 and the market are equally accurate. If E2 has fewer than 30 such events, $s_d$ is estimated from B0 against the market on all belief-determined markets in E2's window and the year before it (B0 needs no assimilation, so it can be run over the longer period); if that also has fewer than 30 events, H12 is demoted to exploratory. From the number of qualifying belief-determined questions listed on Kalshi and Polymarket in the 90 days before freezing, also recorded in the protocol file, we project the time to reach $n^\ast$. If that projection exceeds 24 months, H12 is demoted to exploratory before any E3 score is seen. H12 is analysed once, when $n^\ast$ events have resolved. Brier-score decompositions estimated on small samples are biased~\cite{ferrofricker2012}, so none is reported before $n^\ast$. We expect H12 to be hard: LLM forecasters with retrieval near or occasionally exceed aggregates of human forecasters~\cite{halawi2024} but trail expert forecasters~\cite{karger2024}, LLMs aggregate information more slowly than markets near resolution~\cite{yang2025prophet}, LLMs remain less accurate than markets even when the market is evaluated at each model's training cutoff~\cite{douven2026}, and a simulated population is a less direct forecaster than they are. Failing H12 while passing H13 would show that assimilation improves the simulation without supporting any claim of matching markets. If H10 is not rejected, E3 still runs A1 as a research arm beside the product's filter (B7), and both are scored.

\subsection{Compute}
Equation~\eqref{eq:fj} is a sparse update on an $N\times K$ array. In double precision, 50 members of $10^5$ agents and 20 topics occupy $50\times10^5\times20\times8$ bytes, about $0.8$ GB, plus the graph, so the ensemble fits on one machine; the 14-day snapshot buffer for asynchronous poll operators adds about 5.6 GB in single precision. The LETKF inverts $M\times M$ matrices per block, and $L_0$ for B4 costs $O(NKM^2)$, so the full-size per-agent baseline is affordable. The $M=500$ diagnostic needs about 8 GB per snapshot. LLM cost scales with the number of stories and readings per story, not with agents or members; the display world adds one call per agent per day and is not run in E2 or E3. Measured costs will be reported with E1--E3.

\section{Limitations and failure modes}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The numeric model is a stand-in.} Everything here is assimilation into Equation~\eqref{eq:fj}. Whether LLM agents updated by write-back stay coherent with their own histories is only checked in an exploratory measurement. The fraternal twin tests one kind of model misspecification. Inflation and anchor offsets absorb some systematic error but can also absorb real signal and hide model failure, so offsets will be reported, not only used.

\paragraph{The default lift is justified only in a window.} Propositions~\ref{prop:lift} and~\ref{prop:noise} together justify $L_1$ only when $\sigma_\zeta/\sigma_\eta$ lies between two bounds that depend on segment size, loading--weight mismatch and how many segments an observation aggregates. For small segments the window is nearly empty. E1 measures where the forecast ensemble sits; if it sits outside the window, neither $L_1$ nor a per-agent filter is accurate for individuals, and A1g is the candidate fix.

\paragraph{Wrong spread biases the means.} The default lift does not correct within-segment spread. Because shares depend on spread, a population whose spread is collapsed or inflated will have its segment means moved to wrong values to fit observed shares, and this bias passes into forecasts and forks. This is the failure the paper's motivation names (LLM populations misstate spread), and the default method does not fix it; only A2, where ordinal data exist, and the response-level correction of~\cite{rw_variance} address it. The E1 spread-mismatch arm measures the bias.

\paragraph{Individuals are not observed, and anchors are frozen.} The method preserves within-segment structure; it does not validate it (Remark~1). Errors in anchors, susceptibilities or segment boundaries pass through untouched, and segment-level agreement with polls can coexist with badly wrong individuals. Anchors are never updated, so within-segment anchor bias, the main carrier of the skew toward Western opinion within a segment, is never corrected; only the segment offset $b$ absorbs its segment-level part. The default lift depends on loadings estimated from the forecast ensemble, which depend in turn on susceptibilities that nothing observes. Claims about subgroups smaller than the segments that observations constrain are prior-driven.

\paragraph{Sparse and coarse observation.} In countries with few polls the analysis stays close to the free run, which inherits the skew of LLM-seeded populations~\cite{durmus2023,tao2024}. H4 tests only that assimilation does no harm there, not that it helps. Small countries are usually observed only through national toplines, so their segments are constrained only through the country aggregate, and at the 100-agent floor the finite-population error is comparable to a poll's own sampling error. In large countries, national polls update many segment means through sample covariances whose noise grows with the number of segments (H14). Every published quantity carries its observation density.

\paragraph{Operator misspecification.} Question wording, house effects, mode effects, turnout and the mapping from a stated response to a latent position are modelled crudely. The asynchronous poll operator assumes near-linear evolution of ensemble perturbations over the fieldwork-to-release lag. The market operator applies to few markets, its turnout model is unvalidated, it ignores uncertainty in the reversion rate, and its drift variance comes from historical poll variability that may not hold in the forecast period. Search offsets can drift faster than the filter learns them, as in the Google Flu failure~\cite{lazer2014}. Biased operators produce confidently wrong analyses, and only held-out evaluation detects them.

\paragraph{Gaussian and structural assumptions.} The ETKF assumes near-Gaussian, near-linear errors in the reduced space. Polarized segments are bimodal, which segment means describe poorly; finer segmentation by attitude cluster helps at the cost of sparser observations per segment. Proposition~\ref{prop:lift} assumes one segment factor and independent idiosyncratic noise; the social graph correlates neighbours' $\zeta$. Block-averaged $\theta$ has an uncalibrated spread.

\paragraph{News is not exogenous.} Coverage responds to opinion as well as shaping it. Treating news as forcing therefore biases the estimated gain $\lambda$ toward attributing to news what opinion caused. Shared reading errors across members are not represented by $\Sigma_F$, and coverage gaps in GDELT are invisible to the filter.

\paragraph{LLM inputs are not reproducible from their APIs.} Anchors, every story reading (including the repeated readings behind $\Sigma_F$), the poll- and odds-story classifier outputs and the exact model version strings will be committed as artifacts under \nolinkurl{data/belief-assimilation/llm_cache/}, with their hashes in the protocol file. E1--E3 analyses read only this cache, so anyone can rerun them without calling a model. Over E3's horizon of up to 24 months, providers will deprecate models. The protocol names a successor for each model role; on deprecation the successor is used from the next day, the switch is logged as a deviation with its date, and H12 and H13 are reported pooled (primary) and split by period (sensitivity).

\paragraph{Forecasting is not counterfactual validity.} A filter tuned to predict observed aggregates does not identify how the population would respond to an intervention. Forks of the world with injected events, a central product feature, are therefore not validated by anything in this protocol.

\paragraph{Twenty propositions.} The state compresses what each person believes into about twenty topic positions. Beliefs not tied to those topics, and the connections between topics beyond their within-segment correlations, are not represented.

\paragraph{Ensemble pathologies.} Underdispersion and filter divergence are the classic EnKF failures~\cite{houtekamer2016}; spread--skill monitoring in E1--E3 is the detector. Regime probabilities can collapse onto one regime; the Markov switching prior of the interacting multiple model construction~\cite{blom1988} limits this but does not prevent it.

\paragraph{Reflexivity.} Published forecasts can move markets and, at scale, opinion, which would make the observations depend on the forecasts. We do not assume this is small. Each forecast is committed before its call time, and the scored price is the call-time snapshot, but forecasts published earlier on related questions can move that price. E3 monitors this with an event study of market prices in the hour after each publication, compared with matched times without publication. Decision rule: if the mean absolute price move after publication exceeds that at matched times by more than one percentage point with two-sided $p<0.05$, questions with any related publication before their call time are flagged, H12 is reported with and without them, and from then on related forecasts are published only after the call time of every open question on the same event.

\section{Ethics statement}
\label{sec:ethics}
\paragraph{Manipulation and the fork feature.} Belief assimilation fits a model of what populations of real countries believe and how news moves them. The same machinery could be used to search for messages that shift a target population. The product built on it lets anyone fork the day's world with an injected event and read segment-level shifts, and iterating on forks is a form of message testing. The released system must therefore meet the requirements of~\cite{rw_governing}. Forks accept \emph{shocks}, changes in the state of the world that happen to everyone, not \emph{messages}, communication aimed at a population whose response is then compared. Following~\cite{rw_governing}, announcements by political actors and claims circulating in the political domain count as messages, so ``Candidate X announces Y'' is refused. An event-text classifier decides this and fails closed: a request it cannot classify, or that arrives while it is unavailable, is refused. Forks are rate-limited per account and logged. Their outputs are aggregates over a fixed segment lattice above a size floor, with a typed uncertainty statement. No interface accepts batches of message variants or ranks messages by effect. Because forks are not validated as causal predictions (\S\ref{sec:limits}), their outputs are labelled as model scenarios.

\paragraph{Elections.} We adopt the election rules of~\cite{rw_governing}: an election-window mode with widened intervals around each scheduled election, no forecasts on election day, and a completed per-country blackout review before any forecast about that country's election is published. Many countries embargo pre-election poll publication~\cite{esomarwapor2023}, and the review checks the country's rule. An E3 question about an election in a country without a completed review is not forecast and is recorded as excluded.

\paragraph{Data and licensing.} Agents are synthetic. No agent's belief vector is a statement about a real person, and no identifiable individual is modelled. IPUMS International~\cite{ipums2025} restricts use to scholarly research and teaching, prohibits commercial use and redistribution, and prohibits re-identification~\cite{ipumsterms}; this system is commercial, so it does not use IPUMS International microdata. The demographic skeleton is the GLOPOP-S dataset, released under CC BY 4.0~\cite{ton2024glopopdata}; the article describing it is licensed separately~\cite{ton2024glopop} and reports that it was built in part from Luxembourg Income Study data whose access is restricted. Its status for commercial reuse is therefore pending written confirmation, as recorded in~\cite{rw_unequally}. The commercial terms of the attitude surveys used for anchors (WVS, Pew, Gallup and regional barometers) and the redistribution terms of Kalshi and Polymarket price data are not yet confirmed. The loader refuses any source whose license row is unresolved. If market prices cannot be republished, the public scoreboard will publish market identifiers and call times so that third parties can retrieve prices themselves.

\paragraph{Misrepresentation and targeting.} Group-level outputs can misrepresent groups, and LLMs are known to flatten identity groups~\cite{wang2024flatten}. We publish observation density and calibration per country with every result and state when a region is effectively unassimilated. Modelled beliefs for countries without free polling cannot be checked against data and could be presented as if they were measurements. A country is treated as without free polling if the 2023 ESOMAR/WAPOR worldwide survey of polling freedom codes it as reporting no election polls~\cite{esomarwapor2023}, or if no included poll series fielded a poll there in the 24 months before the window. For such countries we publish only country-level aggregates labelled as unassimilated, and no outputs for segments defined by attitude cluster. The same segment floor applies to every published output.

\paragraph{Conflict of interest.} The authors build a commercial system and evaluate it against markets they could trade on. A policy that neither the company nor its staff trades on any market used as a benchmark must be published with the protocol file; E3 does not start until it is. It is enforced by a compliance owner named in the published policy, with staff attestations published quarterly. As of this draft the policy has not been adopted. Forecasts are committed and timestamped before call time under~\cite{rw_receipts}, and every miss is published.

\section{Conclusion}
We have framed the daily correction of large synthetic belief populations as a data assimilation problem and given a method that respects its two defining constraints: observations are only aggregates, and individual heterogeneity must survive the update. The method updates a reduced state with a localized ensemble transform filter, evaluates observation operators on full populations, and lifts the analysis back to agents. We have characterized when the lift is close to the exact per-agent Kalman update, quantified the noise a finite-ensemble per-agent update adds, and shown that together these justify the default lift only in a window of forecast-spread structure, which the synthetic experiment measures. Whether any of this improves on a free run, the system's current filter, a segment-level model, a poll aggregator or a market price is an empirical question this paper does not answer. It specifies in advance how the question will be answered and what will count as failure.

\section{Inputs from companion work}
\label{app:inputs}
\paragraph{Population, anchors and segments~\cite{rw_unequally}.} Agents are sampled per country from the GLOPOP-S demographic skeleton~\cite{ton2024glopopdata}. Country $k$ receives $n_k=f+\mathrm{LR}\big[(N-fC)\,P_k/\sum_jP_j\big]$ agents, where $P_k$ is its frame population, $C\le237$ the number of included countries, $f=100$ the floor, $N=10^5$, and LR largest-remainder rounding. With $C=237$ a country with population share $p_k$ receives about $100+76{,}300\,p_k$ agents (design arithmetic): about 860 at a 1\% share and about 180 at 0.1\%. Only countries with more than about 1.2\% of the frame population reach $10^3$ agents. Attitude anchors $a_i$ come from published survey aggregates by donor matching on demographics, mapped to each topic's reference-item scale. Attitude anchors are donor-matched only for sources cleared in writing; until then they are set from published aggregates as in~\cite{rw_askaworld}. Segments here are cells of country $\times$ coarse demographics $\times$ attitude cluster, merged until each has at least 50 agents, a coarser partition than the v1 lattice; a floor country holds at most two segments. $C$ and the resulting distribution of segment sizes are computed by these rules and recorded in the protocol file. Each country has a population tier (1--4), which sets the prior standard deviation $\sigma_{b,\tau}$ of its anchor offsets.
\paragraph{Susceptibility.} Nothing observes $w_i$ directly. It is a prior, $w_i=\mathrm{sigmoid}(\alpha_0+\alpha^\top z_i^{\mathrm{att}})$, with $z_i^{\mathrm{att}}$ standardized survey covariates of political interest and attention to news; Appendix~\ref{app:deferred} gives the rule for $(\alpha_0,\alpha)$. Its uncertainty enters the ensemble through $\sigma_w$ (\S\ref{sec:init}); E1 includes a sensitivity analysis on $\sigma_w$.
\paragraph{Commitment~\cite{rw_receipts}.} Each day's forecasts are salted leaves of a Merkle tree whose root is hash-chained to the previous day. Each day record is signed and must be shown, by RFC~3161 time-stamp tokens from a majority of three named authorities, an OpenTimestamps Bitcoin attestation and an entry in a public transparency log, to have existed by a call time fixed in advance by a public schedule; the market reference is read from venue history at that call time. A hash proves integrity; the timestamps prove existence before the call time only under stated clock assumptions; and without independent witnesses, parallel private chains are not prevented.
\paragraph{Questions~\cite{rw_bench}.} Questions are chosen by a mechanical rule from public venue listings and committed before any forecast; this paper forecasts only the belief-determined subset.

\section{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:noise}}
\label{app:proof}
Write $\widehat{\Cov}(a,Y)=\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_m(a^{(m)}-\bar a)(Y^{(m)}-\bar Y)^\top$, a row vector in $\R^{1\times p}$. By Equation~\eqref{eq:factor}, $x_i^{(m)}-\bar x_i=\ell_i(\eta^{(m)}-\bar\eta)+(\zeta_i^{(m)}-\bar\zeta_i)$, so $\widehat{\Cov}(x_i,Y)=\ell_i\widehat{\Cov}(\eta,Y)+\widehat{\Cov}(\zeta_i,Y)$ and $e_i=\widehat{\Cov}(\zeta_i,Y)(C_Y+R)^{-1}$. Since $\sum_m(Y^{(m)}-\bar Y)=0$, $\widehat{\Cov}(\zeta_i,Y)=\frac{1}{M-1}\sum_m\zeta_i^{(m)}(Y^{(m)}-\bar Y)^\top$. Conditional on the $Y^{(m)}$, the $\zeta_i^{(m)}$ are independent of them, mean zero, variance $\sigma_\zeta^2$ and independent over $m$, so this row vector has mean zero and covariance $\sigma_\zeta^2\sum_m(Y^{(m)}-\bar Y)(Y^{(m)}-\bar Y)^\top/(M-1)^2=\sigma_\zeta^2C_Y/(M-1)$. Independence across agents follows from independence of the $\zeta_i$. The mean increment of agent $i$ is $\hat K_id=\ell_i\widehat{\Cov}(\eta,Y)(C_Y+R)^{-1}d+e_id$; the first term is common to all agents up to the factor $\ell_i$, and the second has, given $Y$ and $d$, variance $\frac{\sigma_\zeta^2}{M-1}\,d^\top(C_Y+R)^{-1}C_Y(C_Y+R)^{-1}d$, independent across agents. Taking the expectation over $d$ with $\E\,dd^\top=C_Y+R$ gives $\frac{\sigma_\zeta^2}{M-1}\tr\big((C_Y+R)^{-1}C_Y\big)$, which for $p=1$ is $k\sigma_\zeta^2/(M-1)$. The approximation is in neglecting the dependence of $Y$ on each $\zeta_i$ through $g_i$, which is $O(1/n_s)$.

\section{Quantities fixed after this paper, and the rules that fix them}
\label{app:deferred}
Each item below is fixed by the stated rule before the protocol file is hashed; where the rule is a judgment, one sentence gives its rationale. Nothing else is left free.
\begin{description}
\setlength\itemsep{1pt}
\item[Window.] E2 starts 30 days after the latest training cutoff of any model used and after the end of fieldwork of every wave used for anchors, item maps or $q_e$; it ends on the freeze date.
\item[Poll series and items.] Every recurring multi-country series whose licence row permits use and that has at least three waves before the window (for item linking). An item is assigned to a topic by two coders blind to the window's data; items they disagree on are excluded.
\item[Countries.] E2: every country with at least one held-out poll in the test split. E3: every country with a qualifying question that passes the election rule of \S\ref{sec:ethics}.
\item[Localization radius grid.] Geographic graph hops $\{0,1,2,3\}$ crossed with topic-distance cut-offs $\{0.3,0.6\}$.
\item[Prior and noise scales.] $\sigma_{b,\tau}$: per tier, the root-mean-square error of segment-level anchor means against held-out survey waves in~\cite{rw_unequally}'s validation. $\sigma_\nu$: the root-mean-square error of individual anchors against held-out respondents in the same validation. $\sigma_w=0.3$ (judgment: nothing observes $w$; E1 halves and doubles it). $\sigma_\xi$: grid $\{0.02,0.05,0.1\}$ log-odds per day, chosen by innovation consistency.
\item[Susceptibility coefficients.] $\alpha$ gives equal weight to each standardized attention covariate, and $\alpha_0$ is set so that the population median of $w$ is $0.5$ (judgment: no data identify the scale, which $\lambda$ absorbs).
\item[Market intercept prior.] Standard deviation $0.1$ on the logit scale (judgment: small enough that one series cannot be explained away by its own intercept).
\item[Liquidity threshold.] The median volume of qualifying questions in the 90 days before freezing.
\item[$L_3$ switch (A2).] Sarle's bimodality coefficient above $5/9$, the value for a uniform distribution.
\item[Poll- and odds-story filter.] A fixed LLM classifier prompt and keyword list, committed in the protocol and validated on 500 hand-coded pre-window stories; unscorable stories are removed.
\item[Turnout propensities.] The country's latest national turnout times age-band relative turnout from published aggregates; uniform within a country where those are missing.
\item[$q_e$.] Variance of day-to-day changes in the item's pre-window poll series in that country, pooled across countries in the item family if the country has fewer than 20 polls.
\item[Search offset step.] One tenth of the salience step (judgment: separates slow drift from fast salience; E1 reports their posterior correlation).
\item[Thresholds in tests.] VR band $[0.8,1.25]$: within-segment standard deviation changed by at most about 12\%. Spearman margin $0.02$: for a correlation near $0.8$, a drop of $0.02$ corresponds to added independent noise of about 5\% of within-segment variance. H4 bound $1.05$: at most 5\% worse analysis RMSE than no assimilation where nothing is observed. H7 threshold $0.5$: the loading model explains half the slope variance beyond sampling noise. H9 margin $0.05$ nats: the loss from a mean bias of about $0.32$ predictive standard deviations. H12 relative margin $5\%$: see H12. Leakage threshold $2\%$: about one retained odds report in fifty stories (judgment). E2 floor of 200 polls and 10 countries: the 10 countries make the smallest attainable $p$ fall below every level in the graph. E3 limit of 24 months: beyond that, model deprecations and venue changes make one pre-registered configuration implausible (judgment). E1 truth perturbation levels ($0.3$ segment-level, half the within-segment standard deviation individually, $0.3$ in $\log w$): judgment, with the matched-statistics and spread-mismatch arms reported beside them.
\end{description}
